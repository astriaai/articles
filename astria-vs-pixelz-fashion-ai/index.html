Astria vs Pixelz (2026): Which Is Better for Fashion Brands?

August 25, 2026 · 14 min read
Astria Team
AI fashion production

Pixelz is not another AI image generator, and treating it as one misses the decision. It is a technology-enabled production company: you send product images, expert retouchers and automated steps finish them, and files come back on a guaranteed turnaround. Astria is the opposite arrangement—a production system your own team operates, where the brand's creative direction becomes a reusable asset instead of a specification held by a supplier.

The short answer: Astria is the clear overall winner for fashion brands that want to own a repeatable production capability. Choose Pixelz when you specifically want expert human retouching delivered on an SLA and have no internal team to run production.

Pixelz: outsourced finishing, industrialised​

Pixelz describes itself as one place to scale ecommerce content, and the offering is broad: PDP image retouching, editorial and campaign retouching, video post-production, image-to-video, AI-generated content, and AI models and digital twins. What holds it together is the delivery model. Pixelz's own terms describe the standard service as human-assisted hybrid editing—expert retouchers combined with automated AI-driven steps—which is an honest description of a service rather than a product.

The operational apparatus is real and mature. Customers get a cloud platform for review, markup, order tracking, and dashboards; the Professional plan publishes next-morning turnaround with a three-hour expedite option and capacity around 200 images a day; the Enterprise plan offers turnaround from three hours with custom capacity. Editorial work is quoted at 48 hours standard, video at 24. Brand logos on the site include large retailers such as Zalando, Mango, ASICS, and Bestseller.

The commercial structure deserves a careful read. Professional lists roughly $0.95–$1.35 per image on top of a $75–$95 monthly subscription; Enterprise carries a $1,995–$2,495 monthly subscription with custom per-image rates. Software add-ons run $19–$45 a month each, and professional services range from $195 for product training to $4,995 for advanced onboarding. Every line is reasonable on its own; together they describe a relationship, not a purchase.

Astria: a professional fashion-production environment​

Astria puts the same capability inside the brand. Products, casting, references, templates, and outputs live in a workspace the team controls, and the approved treatment—casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, format—is stored as a reusable recipe rather than a brief sent to a supplier.

That changes what a season looks like. Templates cover beauty, jewelry, footwear, sports, luxury, and editorial work alongside apparel, so campaign and catalog needs come from one setup. Motion extends the same approved stills rather than becoming a separate order. And review happens on a multiplayer Board with named cursors and live voice, where the people making the creative decision are looking at the same canvas rather than annotating returned files.

Public proof: service references versus production adoption​

Pixelz's evidence is enterprise retail scale: recognizable logos and a published case study with luxury retailer Luisa Via Roma. As with every vendor in this comparison, these are first-party materials rather than independent benchmarks. They do establish that large operations trust Pixelz with volume, which is the relevant proof for a service business.

Astria's public materials show a production base among brands and the professionals who create for them—Ronny Kobo, Nununu, Gant, Lee Cooper, Nine West—alongside studios and photographers running client work. Also vendor-published. The two point at different buyers: Pixelz's customer has outsourced production and wants it run well; Astria's customer wants production capability in the building.

A realistic workflow comparison: the mid-season direction change​

Consider a brand three weeks into a 400-SKU season. The catalog imagery is half delivered when the creative director decides the crop is wrong: the products need more headroom and a warmer background, across everything shipped so far and everything still to come.

With a managed service, this is a commercial event. The specification is revised with the supplier, the delivered assets are re-ordered for correction, and the remaining queue is updated. The turnaround is contractually good—next morning on the Professional tier—but the volume doubles for the affected batch, per-image charges apply again, and the brand's own team spends the week coordinating rather than creating. The decision was made in-house; the execution of it was not.

With Astria, the change is an edit. The approved treatment is updated once, the affected SKUs re-run against it, and the same change automatically applies to everything still to come—including the campaign frames and social crops derived from the same direction. The cost is generation, not re-procurement, and the correction happens in the room where the decision was made.

The differences that matter most​

Expert human retouching on a guaranteed turnaround — winner: Pixelz​

This is Pixelz's genuine edge and the one concession that matters here. Skilled retouchers working to a documented specification with a contractual delivery time is a service Astria does not offer. For brands with complex finishing standards, legacy imagery to correct, or no internal production capacity at all, that is a real answer, and the published SLAs and expedite options make it a dependable one. It is a service-delivery advantage rather than a production-capability verdict—you are buying finished files, and that remains true after the hundredth order.

Where the creative direction lives — winner: Astria​

With a service, the brand's visual standard becomes a specification the supplier maintains. It works well, and it is not portable: leaving means rebuilding it, and changing it means renegotiating scope. In Astria the standard is a template in the brand's own workspace, editable by the brand's own people, and it carries into every channel that starts from it.

Coverage across catalog and campaign — winner: Astria​

Pixelz covers a lot: PDP retouching, editorial finishing, video post, AI content. But each is a service line with its own quote, turnaround, and intake. Astria produces catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion from a single approved treatment, which is why the season reads as one shoot rather than four workstreams that were separately briefed.

Collaboration and review — winner: Astria​

Pixelz's platform offers review, markup, and order tracking, which is exactly right for approving work someone else produced. Astria's multiplayer Board is for producing it together: named cursors, live voice, and the actual production canvas, with the photographer, the in-house studio, and the agency in the same workspace.

Collection scale — winner: Astria​

Pixelz's scale is throughput through a production queue, bounded by plan capacity and turnaround windows. Astria's scale is the collection as the unit of work: import the SKU set from Shopify or Google Drive, run it against the approved setup, extend it into campaign and motion. No intake form, no queue position, no per-asset ticket.

Commercial model for brands — winner: Astria​

Pixelz's per-image rate is transparent, and it is only one component: the monthly platform subscription, module add-ons, and onboarding services stack on top, and every revision is billable volume. Astria's brand pricing is scoped to usage—pay for what you create, sized to the production engagement. This article makes no claim that Astria is cheaper; the point is that a per-image rate plus a subscription plus onboarding is not a production budget. Model a full collection, including the mid-season change, before comparing costs.

Compounding production value — winner: Astria​

This is the whole argument in one line. After a year with a managed service, a brand has a large archive of finished images and a supplier relationship. After a year with Astria, it has the images plus the casting, references, templates, and approvals that produced them—and the next season starts from those rather than from a purchase order.

Choose Pixelz if...​

  • You need expert human retouching to a complex standard, with a contractual turnaround.
  • You have no internal production team and do not intend to build one.
  • Legacy image correction at volume is a significant part of the work.
  • Buying finished files is the outcome you want, and owning the workflow is not.

Choose Astria if...​

  • You want production capability inside the brand rather than a supplier relationship.
  • One approved creative direction must carry across catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion.
  • Mid-season creative changes should be an edit, not a re-order.
  • A creative director, photographer, or agency needs to install a workflow other people can reuse.
  • Review should happen on a shared canvas with voice, not on returned files.

The bottom line​

For fashion brands, Astria is the stronger choice: it converts art direction into infrastructure the brand owns, and it makes the next season cheaper in effort rather than in invoices. Pixelz is a mature, well-run outsourced finishing operation—and the right answer when buying finished files, rather than building capability, is genuinely the goal.

The honest test is not image quality. It is what you have at the end of the year.

What to test before choosing either platform​

Take five representative SKUs and run them through both paths, then deliberately change the creative direction after approval. Measure what the change costs in each: money, elapsed time, and your own team's hours. Price the full commercial picture for the service option—subscription, per-image charges, add-on modules, onboarding—not just the headline rate. Have a senior creative review outputs blind for garment fidelity, drape, anatomy, styling, and brand fit. Finally, ask what you would keep if you switched vendors tomorrow.

Frequently asked questions​

Is Pixelz an Astria alternative?​

They solve the same business problem in opposite ways. Pixelz is a managed service: you send assets and receive finished files on an SLA. Astria is a production system your team operates, where the creative direction and the outputs stay in-house.

Does Pixelz still require a photoshoot?​

Its core business is post-production on images you supply, though it also offers AI-generated content, AI models, and digital twins as services. Astria produces imagery from the product photography you already have, inside a workflow your team runs.

Which is more predictable to budget?​

Pixelz publishes per-image rates, but the total stacks a monthly platform subscription, per-image charges, optional module fees, and onboarding services. Astria's brand pricing is scoped to usage. Model a full collection, including rework, before comparing costs.

What happens when the creative direction changes mid-season?​

With a managed service, a direction change means revising a specification and re-ordering affected assets through the queue. In Astria, you edit the approved treatment and re-run the collection against it.

Is Astria better than Pixelz for fashion brands?​

For brands that want to own a repeatable production capability, yes. Brands that specifically want expert human retouching delivered on a guaranteed turnaround, with no internal team to run it, should evaluate Pixelz for that service.
