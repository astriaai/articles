Astria vs StyTrix: Pricing & Fashion Workflow Review (2026)

August 23, 2026 · 7 min read
Astria Team
AI fashion production

StyTrix and Astria overlap in fashion image generation, but the brief matters more than the overlap. A designer developing a garment needs room to change it. An ecommerce team photographing an approved garment needs it to stay the same.

The short answer: shortlist StyTrix for design development and test Astria for the collection's final imagery. StyTrix's saved assets make the comparison closer than a simple “design tool versus production tool” label suggests. Start with the pricing table, then test the handoff from one approved look to the rest of the collection.

The pricing page lists a three-day paid-plan trial, Pro credit rollover, and non-expiring top-up packs. Pro includes up to three seats; Team includes up to ten. API and batch processing appear under Enterprise. Annual billing is a separate commitment.

There is a documentation conflict: the user guide shows different plan prices and trial wording. Use the dedicated pricing page for the table above and check the checkout terms before subscribing.

How far do StyTrix credits go?​

The guide lists Concept Creator and Mix & Match at ten credits per generation. For those operations, 240 generations require 2,400 credits. That is 900 beyond the Team allowance, before additional attempts. This is illustrative credit arithmetic, not a quote for 240 approved images; other operations and output settings need their own calculation.

Measure cost per SKU after review, rather than treating every generation as a finished product photo.

StyTrix review: the design room, digitised​

StyTrix documents sketch rendering, outfit assembly, image-to-sketch, split layers, and style training. Its guide also describes reusable model, fabric, and style libraries, plus shared-canvas comments. Those capabilities are described in the StyTrix user guide.

Our assessment: these tools are a sensible starting point when the expensive loop is trying ideas, discussing them, and changing the design. The production test comes later. A garment that looks persuasive in a proposal still needs to match the approved sample before it appears on a product page.

Astria: producing imagery from approved products​

Astria's fashion offering centres on product-photo inputs and templates for lookbook, campaign, and social imagery. Its public API documentation provides a separate route for technical teams.

The practical reason to evaluate it is repeatability. Approve a hero image, record the casting and treatment, then require the next operator to produce the next look. Our AI lookbook guide sets out that production sequence. Whether a particular tool preserves the details well enough is something the pilot must establish.

A realistic workflow comparison: the day the collection is approved​

Imagine a 60-style capsule requiring four images per style. Before production, decide which images are product records and which are editorial. A front view that changes a closure is a reject even if it makes a better campaign image.

Run five representative styles first: a plain garment, a print, a layered look, a reflective trim, and the hardest silhouette. Keep a record of the inputs, settings, credits, manual edits, and approved outputs. Then add a colourway and hand the work to a second operator. That last step tells you whether the treatment can actually be reused.

A subscription allowance is a budgeting constraint, not proof that the platform cannot handle a collection. Compare top-ups or a larger plan with the cost of moving the job elsewhere. Include the time it takes to prepare and review the assets in either system.

Choose StyTrix if...​

  • The brief starts with a sketch or an unfinished design.
  • The team needs to compare silhouettes, fabrics, and outfit combinations before approving a sample.
  • Its canvas and saved libraries fit how your designers review work.

Choose Astria if...​

  • Your inputs are approved products and your deliverables are publishable fashion images.
  • You want to build the lookbook production around an approved hero treatment.
  • You want to evaluate a documented API alongside the visual workflow.

What to test before choosing either platform​

Require a product image, an editorial frame, and a repeatable second look from the same inputs. Check garment construction at full size and compare the whole set for casting and colour drift. Record approvals and rejects. Neither a rendered sketch nor a saved style is proof of garment fidelity.

Frequently asked questions​

Is StyTrix an Astria alternative?​

There is overlap in fashion image generation. Evaluate StyTrix for design development and Astria for producing imagery from approved products. Test the complete brief before choosing.

Does StyTrix save models and styles?​

Yes. Its user guide describes model, fabric, and style libraries. Test how those saved assets behave across your own collection rather than assuming each image starts from scratch.

What should a StyTrix pricing comparison include?​

Include the required operations, additional attempts, seats, storage, and automation access. Compare the total against approved deliverables rather than raw image count.
