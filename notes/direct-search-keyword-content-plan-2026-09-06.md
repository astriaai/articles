# Direct-search keyword research and content plan

**Research date:** September 6, 2026  
**Market lens:** English-language search, with a live Google results check from Israel and broader web-result sampling  
**Scope:** Astria's fashion and ecommerce image-production content

## Executive decision

The strongest uncovered search topic is **AI product photography**. Astria has pages about AI-versus-studio, costs, prompts, platforms, and individual verticals, but it does not have one canonical guide that owns the head query and routes readers to those pages.

The strongest transactional phrase is **AI fashion model generator**. That query should be owned by a product or use-case landing page, not by another blog article. Google's result page is dominated by tools, and its related searches are mostly `free`, `online`, and `app` variants. The existing `ai-fashion-models` article is a useful casting and rights guide, but it does not satisfy someone looking for a generator.

The recommended order is therefore:

1. Specify an **AI fashion model generator** product/use-case page outside the articles repo.
2. Refresh three existing articles around direct search language: `flat-lay-to-on-model`, `best-ai-fashion-photography-platforms`, and `3d-packshots-ecommerce`.
3. Publish one new pillar only after a Search Console overlap check: **AI Product Photography for Ecommerce: Workflow, Inputs, and Quality Control**.
4. Hold beauty/skincare, Shopify-specific, and competitor-alternative expansions until first-party query data supports a separate URL.

This keeps the repo's content-pause rule intact: most of the opportunity is better query ownership, not more pages.

## What the live results say

### 1. `AI fashion model generator` is a tool-selection query

The live Google page led with product pages and tool comparisons. Its related searches included:

- `ai fashion model generator free`
- `ai fashion model generator online`
- `AI put clothes on model free`
- `AI fashion model video generator free`
- `AI fashion model generator free app`
- `AI fashion model app`

The ranking pages define the job consistently: upload a garment or flat lay, place it on a generated model, and create ecommerce-ready on-model images. Examples include [Botika](https://botika.com/), [Photoroom](https://www.photoroom.com/tools/virtual-model), [Claid's tool comparison](https://claid.ai/blog/article/ai-fashion-model-generators), and [On-Model's buyer guide](https://on-model.com/blog/best-ai-fashion-model-generators).

**Implication:** a searcher wants to try or choose a tool. A casting essay is supporting content, not the best primary result. Do not force the existing article to own the transactional query and do not create a second overlapping blog guide.

### 2. `AI product photography` is a missing pillar

The head-query results mix comprehensive guides, product pages, and visual galleries. Shopify's current guide answers definition, workflow, tools, best practices, and FAQs on one URL. Kive ranks a large example gallery. Product vendors create category pages for both general and fashion-specific versions. See [Shopify's guide](https://www.shopify.com/blog/ai-product-photography), [Kive's examples](https://kive.ai/product-shots), and [Photoroom's fashion page](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/fashion).

Astria's current [AI product photography vs studio](https://www.astria.ai/articles/ai-product-photography-vs-studio/) page owns a narrower comparison and hybrid-workflow job. Retitling it to the head term would sacrifice that clear intent. A separate canonical guide is justified if Search Console shows impressions for the head cluster that do not already consolidate on this page.

**Implication:** add one hub, not several near-duplicate definitions. Its job is to explain the production system and route readers to the existing cost, prompt, platform, lookbook, product-to-model, Shopify, Amazon, jewelry, and footwear pages.

### 3. Search language is operational, not abstract

Recurring exact or near-exact tasks include:

- turn a flat lay into an on-model photo;
- put clothes on an AI model;
- change the model but keep the clothing;
- generate multiple product angles from one photo;
- replace a product background without changing the product;
- keep one model and one visual treatment across a catalog;
- create Shopify-ready product images;
- decide whether AI product photography is accurate enough to publish.

The multi-angle result set contains dedicated tools and how-to pages from [Snaproom](https://www.snaproom.app/tools/multi-angle), [Rawshot](https://rawshot.ai/academy/how-to-create-product-side-and-back-views-in-rawshot-ai), and other vendors. The repeated caveat is equally important: unseen sides are inferred, not recovered. Astria already makes this distinction in its 3D and hybrid-studio articles, so the opportunity is to expose that language more clearly rather than add another URL.

Background-replacement results are even more product-led, with exact-match generator pages such as [BulkGen](https://www.bulk-gen.com/use-cases/product-background-generator). That is a landing-page or tool intent, not a good article target unless first-party query data shows a question Astria can answer better.

### 4. Beauty/skincare is the strongest adjacent vertical

Exact-match commercial pages and buyer guides now exist for `AI beauty product photography`, `AI skincare product photography`, and close variants. Current examples include [Photoroom's skincare page](https://www.photoroom.com/ai-product-photography/skincare), [Kive's 2026 buyer guide](https://kive.ai/learn/ai-skincare-and-beauty-product-photography), and [Blend's beauty page](https://www.blendnow.com/industry/beauty-cosmetics).

This is stronger evidence than the handbag or watch results, which are mostly isolated vendor landing pages. Beauty also has a defensible editorial angle: shade accuracy, reflective packaging, label text, skin rendering, and the boundary between campaign imagery and product proof.

**Implication:** beauty is the first expansion candidate after the fashion/product core proves itself. It is not part of the immediate sprint because the current ledger explicitly holds broader backlog growth pending query evidence.

### 5. Alternatives pages are crowded and need a real test

`Photoroom alternatives` has visible commercial competition from multiple exact-match pages, including [Kive](https://kive.ai/learn/photoroom-alternatives), [WearView](https://www.wearview.co/alternatives/photoroom-alternatives), and [Nightjar](https://nightjar.so/blog/best-photoroom-alternatives-product-photography). Search results for Claid, Flair, and Pebblely comparisons were much less consistent and often returned the vendors' own product pages instead of clear comparison demand.

**Implication:** do not restart the broad competitor roster. Consider a Photoroom page only after Search Console or a paid keyword source confirms demand and only if Astria can run a reproducible fashion-product benchmark. Do not publish untested listicles.

## Recommended page briefs

### A. Main-site landing page: AI Fashion Model Generator

**Primary query:** `AI fashion model generator`  
**Search job:** try a tool, see examples, understand inputs, and decide whether it can preserve the garment.

Suggested title:

> AI Fashion Model Generator for Clothing Brands | Astria

Required modules:

1. Interactive or clearly linked start state above the fold.
2. Before/after examples using an actual flat lay or product reference.
3. The four jobs: product-to-model, model swap, consistent cast, and campaign variants.
4. Input guidance for flat lay, ghost mannequin, on-model, and multi-reference sources.
5. A garment-accuracy checklist covering cut, print, logo, closures, texture, and hidden views.
6. Honest boundary: generated wear imagery is not measured fit evidence.
7. Links to the casting guide, flat-lay guide, consistency guide, platform shortlist, and virtual try-on guide.

Do not target `free` unless the live product genuinely offers a stable free path. Do not put `app` in the title unless there is an app experience that satisfies that wording.

### B. New article: AI Product Photography for Ecommerce

**Primary query:** `AI product photography`  
**Secondary:** `product photography AI`, `AI product photos`, `how to create AI product photography`, `AI product photography for ecommerce`

Suggested title:

> AI Product Photography for Ecommerce: Workflow, Inputs, and Quality Control

Suggested slug:

> `ai-product-photography`

The page should answer:

1. What AI product photography is and the four distinct modes: cleanup, background/scene generation, product-to-model, and campaign variation.
2. What source images are needed and what missing views cannot be recovered as fact.
3. A six-step production workflow from canonical reference to channel-ready output.
4. Product-truth QA before creative approval.
5. Which assets should remain physical evidence.
6. Cost per approved image rather than cost per generation.
7. When a generator, a specialist fashion platform, a studio, or a hybrid workflow is the right buy.
8. A visual before/after example for apparel, jewelry, footwear, and one non-fashion object.

Query boundaries:

- Tools and vendors belong to `best-ai-fashion-photography-platforms`.
- Detailed prompts belong to `ecommerce-product-photography-prompts`.
- Studio comparison belongs to `ai-product-photography-vs-studio`.
- Cost formulas belong to `product-photography-cost-per-sku`.
- Marketplace rules belong to the Shopify and Amazon guides.

### C. Existing-page refreshes

#### `flat-lay-to-on-model`

- Add `AI put clothes on a model` to the opening answer and one H2.
- Add a short terminology block mapping `flat lay to model`, `product-to-model`, `clothing on model generator`, and brand-side virtual try-on.
- Add FAQs for model choice, model swap, and source-image requirements.
- Keep the existing accuracy and fit caveats; they are a useful differentiator from generator landing pages.

#### `best-ai-fashion-photography-platforms`

- Add an H2 answering `What is the best AI fashion model generator for a clothing brand?`
- Compare the jobs Google results conflate: one-off model generator, catalog production system, shopper try-on, and generic background tool.
- Add visible test status to each recommendation: hands-on tested, source-reviewed only, or not recently tested.
- Do not claim a winner on garment fidelity without a matched benchmark.

#### `3d-packshots-ecommerce`

- Add an H2 for `Can AI generate multiple product angles from one photo?`
- Explain that visible details can be preserved while hidden geometry is inferred.
- Route true product-proof needs to physical multi-angle capture and campaign/rotation needs to the generated workflow.
- Link to the existing best-services comparison and the AI-versus-studio article.

#### `shopify-product-media-guide`

- Add one section on assembling a Shopify apparel image set from physical evidence plus AI derivatives.
- Cover featured image, on-model context, detail views, alternate angles, and video in gallery order.
- Do not turn the page into an app roundup unless query data shows that is what the URL already attracts.

## Pages not to create now

- A second `AI fashion models` blog article.
- A generic `AI fashion model generator free` article.
- Separate pages for `product on model`, `put clothes on AI model`, and `flat lay to model`; these are one intent cluster.
- Separate articles for every background, crop, pose, or camera-angle task.
- More `Astria vs <vendor>` pages solely because a vendor appeared in a search result.
- Thin handbag, watch, sunglasses, perfume, or supplement pages without Search Console or customer-usage evidence.
- A broad `best AI product photography tools` article that duplicates the fashion-platform shortlist without a meaningfully different audience and test set.

## Search Console validation before publication

Export the last 90 days for `/articles/`, then group queries with these case-insensitive patterns:

```text
ai product photograph|product photograph.*ai
fashion model generator|model generator.*cloth
flat.?lay.*model|product.*on.?model|put cloth.*model
multi.*angle|side.*back.*view|360.*product
shopify.*(photo|image)|(?:photo|image).*shopify
beauty.*photograph|skincare.*photograph|cosmetic.*photograph
photoroom|claid|flair|pebblely
```

Apply the repository's existing decision order:

1. Fix indexation first.
2. Refresh an existing URL when it already earns impressions, especially at positions 11–30.
3. Fix titles/descriptions when impressions exist but CTR is near zero.
4. Create a new URL only for demonstrated unmatched intent.
5. Check paired URLs for cannibalization before expanding either one.

For the proposed `ai-product-photography` pillar, do not publish until the page/query export answers both questions:

- Is the head cluster already consolidating on `ai-product-photography-vs-studio`?
- Are there meaningful impressions for the head cluster with no satisfactory landing page?

If the first answer is yes and the existing page is rising, refresh and broaden it cautiously instead of splitting authority. If the second answer is yes, publish the dedicated pillar and keep the comparison page narrowly titled and internally linked.

## Four-week execution plan

### Week 1 — measurement and query ownership

- Export and cluster Search Console queries.
- Confirm indexation and canonical status for the five target existing URLs.
- Write the main-site AI fashion model generator landing-page specification.
- Refresh `flat-lay-to-on-model` and `3d-packshots-ecommerce` if their query clusters already have impressions.

### Week 2 — commercial investigation

- Refresh `best-ai-fashion-photography-platforms` with direct generator language and test-status disclosure.
- Add the bounded AI section to `shopify-product-media-guide` if the Shopify query cluster is visible.
- Build or select matched before/after assets for the head-term pillar and main-site landing page.

### Week 3 — one new pillar, conditionally

- Publish `ai-product-photography` only if the GSC boundary test supports a new canonical URL.
- Add contextual internal links from the cost, prompts, platform, studio, lookbook, footwear, jewelry, Shopify, and Amazon pages.
- Keep each anchor aligned with its owning intent rather than repeating the same head keyword everywhere.

### Week 4 — evaluate and choose one expansion

- Check early indexation, impressions, CTR, and page/query overlap.
- Choose at most one next move: beauty/skincare, a crawlable examples gallery, or a benchmarked Photoroom alternatives page.
- Do not choose a second move until the first has a measurable search or product-engagement hypothesis.

## Measurement

Track by page and query cluster, not sitewide totals:

- indexed/canonical status;
- impressions and clicks;
- CTR;
- average position;
- number of queries in positions 8–30;
- product CTA clicks from the article;
- overlap: the same query earning impressions on more than one Astria URL;
- assisted movement from the pillar into a product or gallery route.

Compare equal 28-day windows after deployment. The research above establishes current language and intent, not guaranteed volume or ranking. Use Search Console as the release gate and Google Ads Keyword Planner, Ahrefs, or Semrush only to size opportunities that remain ambiguous after first-party data.
