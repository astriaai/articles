Astria vs DRESSX (2026): Which Is Better for Fashion Brands?

August 26, 2026 · 13 min read
Astria Team
AI fashion production

DRESSX and Astria both put clothes on people with AI, and there the overlap ends. DRESSX builds the experience a shopper has at the moment of decision: a fitting room on the product page, an AI stylist, an interactive mirror in the store. Astria builds what fills that page in the first place—the catalog imagery, the lookbook, the campaign, the social cutdowns.

The short answer: for producing a brand's imagery, Astria is the clear winner, and that is the decision this page addresses. For adding a shopper-facing fitting room to a product page or a store, DRESSX is the specialist and Astria does not compete for it. A brand with budget for both is not making a mistake.

How we evaluated Astria and DRESSX​

These products sit on opposite sides of the product page, so we evaluated them against one shared question: what does a brand need in order to publish and merchandise a season? That means asking who each product serves, what it produces, what it measures, and where the creative direction lives — while stating plainly where DRESSX's real value sits, which is at the point of purchase.

DRESSX: the experience at the point of decision​

DRESSX has been in digital fashion since 2020 and has converged on a clear enterprise proposition: an AI suite for fashion and luxury brands, sold as modules. Virtual try-on is the flagship, answering the question the company puts at the center of its pitch—whether an item will actually look good on the shopper. It handles single items, full looks, and mix-and-match, integrates through an API, a white-label option, or a Shopify plugin, and the company advertises very fast integration.

Around it sit adjacent products: an AI customer assistant, an AI stylist for retail, and DRESSX Mirror, a human-sized interactive screen for stores, pop-ups, window displays, and events. Named implementations include Victoria Beckham and Farfetch.

The published outcome claims are aimed squarely at ecommerce leadership: a 40% reduction in return rate, a 3.2× conversion lift, 68% higher engagement, and up to 75% fit certainty. These are vendor-published figures without disclosed methodology, and a buyer should ask how they were measured and over what baseline. But they are the right metrics for what DRESSX sells, and they are not metrics a content production tool should be claiming.

Astria: a professional fashion-production environment​

Astria's job starts earlier in the funnel and further back in the calendar. Products, casting, references, templates, and outputs live in a brand workspace, and the approved treatment—casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, format—becomes the reusable unit that produces the season.

That covers the assets a try-on experience cannot: the PDP images a shopper sees before deciding to engage, the lookbook that frames the collection, the campaign that drives traffic, the social cutdowns, and the motion. Templates span beauty, jewelry, footwear, sports, luxury, and editorial alongside apparel, and the same approved direction runs across the whole SKU set imported from Shopify or Google Drive.

Public proof: storefront metrics versus production adoption​

DRESSX publishes brand implementations and conversion-oriented statistics. Read them as evidence that the experience layer gets deployed by serious retailers, and probe the methodology in a demo.

Astria's public materials show a production base among brands and the professionals who create for them: Ronny Kobo, Nununu, Gant, Lee Cooper, and Nine West, alongside studios and photographers running client work. Also vendor-published. Neither company's evidence speaks to the other's domain, which is the accurate summary of this comparison.

A realistic workflow comparison: what the shopper actually sees​

Trace a single customer journey for a spring dress. She sees a paid-social video, clicks through to a campaign landing page, browses the collection grid, opens the product page, scrolls the four PDP images and a detail shot, then taps "try it on."

DRESSX owns the last step of that journey and does it well. Everything before it—the social video, the campaign frame, the collection grid, the five PDP images—is content the brand had to produce. If the answer to producing it was a photoshoot, DRESSX has not changed the brand's production economics at all; it has improved the conversion rate on assets that still cost what they always cost.

With Astria, that entire upstream chain comes from one approved treatment: the campaign frame, the grid, the PDP set, the detail crop, and the social video all derive from the same casting, styling, and direction, run across the collection. The try-on button at the end is a complementary purchase, not an alternative one.

The differences that matter most​

Shopper-facing try-on, fit confidence, and returns — winner: DRESSX​

This is DRESSX's genuine edge and the one concession that matters. Rendering a specific shopper into a specific garment, at request time, on the product page or an in-store mirror, is a hard problem with direct commercial consequences—and Astria does not attempt it. Brands whose live problem is return rates or fit hesitation should evaluate DRESSX on that basis, ask hard questions about how the published lift figures were measured, and not expect a content production tool to substitute.

Producing what the shopper sees first — winner: Astria​

A fitting room improves the last click. It does not make the campaign, the grid, or the PDP set. Astria produces all of them from a single approved direction, which is why the collection reads as one shoot rather than a series of separately commissioned jobs.

Where the creative direction lives — winner: Astria​

DRESSX renders at request time against whatever imagery and product data the brand supplies; the brand's creative direction is an input it consumes rather than an asset it holds. In Astria the direction is the stored, reusable thing: casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, and format, editable by the brand's own team and applied to everything downstream.

Collaboration and review — winner: Astria​

DRESSX's team surface is integration and merchandising configuration, which is appropriate for an experience layer. Astria's is creative review: private brand workspaces, a multiplayer Board with named cursors, and live voice over the actual canvas, shared by the photographer, in-house studio, and agency.

Collection scale and commercial fit — winner: Astria​

DRESSX scales with catalog coverage on the storefront, and its commercial model is an enterprise integration rather than published pricing. Astria treats the collection as the unit of work—import the SKU set, run it against the approved setup, extend into campaign and motion—with brand pricing scoped to usage: pay for what you create. The two are not substitutes on cost, and this article makes no claim that one is cheaper; they buy different things.

Compounding production value — winner: Astria​

Every season, Astria's workspace holds more: products, avatars, templates, approvals, outputs. A try-on integration does not compound in the same way—it performs the same function on whatever catalog it is pointed at, which is exactly what it should do.

Choose DRESSX if...​

  • Return rates, fit hesitation, or PDP conversion are the problem you are funding.
  • You want a fitting-room experience on the storefront, in an app, or on an in-store screen.
  • An AI stylist or customer assistant fits your retail experience roadmap.
  • Your content production is already solved.

Choose Astria if...​

  • You need to produce the catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion assets for a collection.
  • One approved creative direction must carry across every channel.
  • A creative director, photographer, or agency needs to install a workflow other people can reuse.
  • Review should happen on a shared canvas with voice, not through exported files.
  • Your production cost, not your conversion rate, is the constraint.

The bottom line​

If content production is the bottleneck, Astria is the answer and try-on will not help. If content is handled and conversion is the bottleneck, DRESSX is the answer and a production system will not help. Most brands discover, in order, that they have the first problem — because the fitting room is only as good as the collection page that got the shopper to it.

What to test before choosing either platform​

Map your funnel and count the assets. For every product page you plan to add try-on to, list the campaign, grid, PDP, detail, and social assets it depends on, and note who produces them today and at what cost. Then run five representative SKUs through Astria for that full channel set, and add a colorway after approval. If you are also evaluating DRESSX, ask for the methodology behind its published conversion and returns figures, and for a live implementation you can browse as a shopper.

Frequently asked questions​

Is DRESSX an Astria alternative?​

No — they serve different jobs. DRESSX is a shopper-facing experience layer: virtual try-on, an AI stylist, and an in-store mirror. Astria produces the brand's catalog, lookbook, campaign, and social imagery. A brand can reasonably buy both.

Does virtual try-on replace product photography?​

No. Try-on renders a shopper into a garment at the moment of decision. It does not produce the PDP images, lookbook, campaign frames, or social assets that bring the shopper to that page in the first place.

Which owns conversion and returns?​

DRESSX targets those metrics directly and publishes claims for return reduction and conversion lift. Astria is upstream of them: it produces the imagery a brand merchandises with, across every channel.

Which is better for producing a season's imagery?​

Astria. One approved creative direction produces catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion across the collection. Content production is not what the DRESSX suite is built for.

Is Astria better than DRESSX for fashion brands?​

For producing the brand's imagery, yes, and that is the decision this article addresses. For adding a fitting-room experience to a product page or a store, DRESSX is the specialist and Astria does not compete with it.
