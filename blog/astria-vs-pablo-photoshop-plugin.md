## Pablo: a hosted-credit panel with published rates

Pablo is a UXP plugin for Photoshop 26.0.0 or higher, made in Amsterdam and listed on Adobe Exchange on 4 December 2025. Its [pricing page](https://trypablo.com/pricing) lists Nano Banana, Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana Pro, Seedream 4.0 and 4.5, and GPT Image 1.5, 2 and 2.5. The [Exchange listing](https://exchange.adobe.com/apps/cc/e5ace85b/pablo) describes multi-reference images, 4K generation and "add and replace objects". Its note for version 0.7.46, updated 11 September 2026, adds GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst.

Commercially, Pablo publishes what many rivals do not. The individual licence is $19 one-time; the agency licence is $325 one-time for up to 50 seats, aimed at organisations of ten or more users. Every purchase includes 50 free generations. Per-image prices are published per model and resolution, with no markup over the provider's rate. Nano Banana Pro is listed at $0.15 to $0.17 at 1K or 2K and $0.26 to $0.28 at 4K. Seedream 4.0 is $0.03, GPT Image 2 runs $0.02 to $0.13 by quality, and GPT Image 2.5 is $0.13 at 1K. You can also bring a Google or Replicate key. The pricing page advertises a 14-day refund on the licence.

Two things to read before paying. Pablo's [terms](https://trypablo.com/terms) say prepaid credits expire 24 months after purchase unless otherwise specified at checkout, and one public Exchange review alleges a 90-day window on a balance. Check the expiry shown when you top up. Separately, the public pages do not say what layer type a result lands as, or what pixel dimensions come back when a 4K generation is placed into a small selection.

## Astria: the panel is one surface of a platform

The Astria Nano Banana plugin for Photoshop is a CCX/UXP panel for Photoshop 2025 (version 26 and above) on macOS and Windows. The panel carries GPT Image 2 (mask-aware, at 1K, 2K or 4K), Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana Pro, Seedream 5 Pro at 4K, Seedream 5 with 4K edit, and Flux 2 Pro. Flux Kontext was retired in favour of these. The [landing page](https://www.astria.ai/nano-banana-photoshop) also lists GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst and Flare and Muse Image as available now on the platform.

That last sentence is the first headline. The panel's model list is served by the Astria platform, so a model added to Astria appears in the panel the day it launches, without a plugin update or re-download.

The workflow is three steps. Select with the marquee, lasso, brush or colour range. Prompt in short, specific language. Generate, and the result lands as a new layer. Mask-aware models read the selection directly. Reference images dropped into the panel act as visual instructions. The model is chosen per task from one dropdown, and keys are switched inside the panel.

The third headline is what that layer is. Each generation is placed as a Smart Object holding the model's full returned resolution. A 4K result placed into a small selection still carries the 4K pixels, so it can be re-transformed, masked and re-edited later without resampling.

Billing is $9 one-time. Generations are pay-as-you-go against an Astria key, topped up only when needed, with no subscription and no minimum. One key authenticates every model, and Astria abstracts the Google, OpenAI, fal and ByteDance APIs so quota, auth and retries stay invisible. Or paste a Google AI Studio key and Gemini calls run against your own Google quota. Per-image rates are on the [pricing page](https://www.astria.ai/pricing). No GPU is needed. The older JSX script still works with Replicate credits, and the CCX build was a free upgrade with a $2 starter credit. Sales are final, since the plugin is a downloadable file.

## One campaign hero, then forty SKUs

Take a footwear campaign hero shot on location. The art director wants three edits: the shoes swapped for the new colourway from a product reference, the background extended for a 4:5 crop, and the phone in the model's left hand removed. Then the colourway has to appear on the other forty SKUs in the drop.

### In Pablo

Drop the product reference in, lasso the shoes, and prompt the swap on Nano Banana Pro. Extend the canvas, select the empty band and prompt the fill. Brush over the phone and prompt its removal. Each result returns as a layer, and the cost of every generation is knowable from the published rate card. What the public material does not say is whether the swap layer holds the full 4K return or only the selection's pixel count, or whether it re-transforms later without loss. Test that before committing the hero.

### In the Astria plugin

The same three edits, with a model chosen per task. GPT Image 2 with mask is the natural pick for the shoe swap: it reads the lasso as a mask and is built for clean replacement inside a brushed selection. Seedream 5 handles the 4:5 extension from a longer structural prompt. Nano Banana Pro removes the phone while keeping the hand and sleeve consistent. Each result is a Smart Object at the model's full returned resolution, so when the crop changes next week the swap layer is scaled, re-masked and re-edited without resampling.

### And the other forty SKUs

Pablo is a plugin; forty more SKUs are forty more documents and forty more selections. With Astria, the key in the panel is the same key and balance that run [Astria workspaces](https://www.astria.ai/ecommerce), templates and the API. Photoshop is for the hero and the fix. ## The differences that matter most

### New-model lead time: winner Astria

Pablo is quick. Its Nano Banana Pro how-to went up on Google's launch day, its GPT Image 2 post followed OpenAI by one day, and GPT Image 2.5 arrived three days after OpenAI's 8 September 2026 release. The difference is not speed but mechanism. Pablo's newest model is tied to plugin version 0.7.46. A user who has not updated does not have it, and the roster is bounded by what the plugin build knows about. The Astria panel reads its list from the platform. A model added to Astria appears in the dropdown with no plugin update, which is why GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst and Flare and Muse Image are already listed as available.

### Returned resolution and layer type: winner Astria

Both products advertise 4K. Only one says what happens to those pixels afterwards. Astria places every result as a Smart Object holding the model's full returned resolution. Pablo's layer type and returned pixel dimensions are not stated in its public material, so a buyer has to measure them. For a retoucher who re-crops, re-lights and re-masks a hero across a campaign's life, that is the whole reason to care about the layer type.

### Cost to start: winner Astria

$9 one-time against $19 for an individual seat. Neither product asks for a subscription, and both let a Gemini-heavy user route calls through their own Google key at Google's price. Per image, take neither vendor's word for it: Pablo's rates are on its pricing page and Astria's are on the [pricing page](https://www.astria.ai/pricing). Price the models you actually run, at the resolution you ship.

### Published rates, agency licensing and a free allowance: winner Pablo

This is Pablo's real advantage and it deserves a plain statement. Pablo publishes pass-through per-image rates with no markup. It sells a $325 one-time licence for up to 50 seats with centralised billing and includes 50 free generations on purchase. It runs both the hosted and bring-your-own-key paths, with Replicate as a second key option. An agency buying seats in bulk, whose finance team wants the per-image number before the first generation, will find Pablo's commercial pages easier to sign off.

### Production beyond the document: winner Astria

In the public material, Pablo's account dashboard is for licences and credits, not generation. The Astria key that runs the panel also runs workspaces, templates and the API. The balance you top up for Photoshop produces the forty SKUs, from the same product reference used for the hero. For a studio whose Photoshop work is the visible tenth of a larger production, the panel is the entry point and the [ecommerce workspace](https://www.astria.ai/ecommerce) is the rest.

### Maintenance and longevity: winner Astria

Both products are maintained. Pablo has a dated public record, a support desk and a company behind it, which is more than most Exchange listings in this category can say. Astria's panel was rebuilt as a CCX/UXP plugin in 2026, carries a dated changelog and reports 16,000 sales. It is one surface of a platform company rather than a company's only product. If the question is which panel is still current in eighteen months, the one that needs no release to see a new model has the structural edge.

For everyone else, the Astria Nano Banana plugin for Photoshop is our recommendation. It costs $9. It will have the next model without a release. It keeps every returned pixel in a Smart Object. Run one PSD through both panels with the same selection, reference and short prompt on Nano Banana Pro at 4K. Read the credit terms at checkout, including any expiry, before topping up more than a week's work.

Pablo is a maintained hosted-credit panel with published pass-through rates, a 50-seat agency licence and 50 free generations on purchase. Buy it if those three things decide the purchase.
