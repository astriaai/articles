An apparel product video earns its place when it answers something a shopper cannot confidently resolve from the photographs. Where does the jacket end when someone turns? How does the closure work? What is inside the bag? Each question calls for a different shot—and a different standard of evidence.

**Start with one product, one buying question, and one short edit.** Film the physical behavior you need to prove. Use generated motion selectively for presentation, with the actual SKU as the review reference.

## Write the buying question before the shot list

A product detail page (PDP) video is a small demonstration attached to a specific item for sale. Its brief should name the SKU, colorway, sample size, unanswered question, and action that will answer it. “Make the collection feel cinematic” belongs in the [AI fashion film guide](./ai-fashion-film-guide.md). “Show how this jacket opens” belongs here.

Read customer questions, return reasons, and support notes for the selected product family. Pick a question motion can actually resolve. A shopper asking about sleeve length may need measurements and a fit photograph before they need a video.

| Buying question | Useful shot | Evidence needed |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Where does this jacket fall from the side? | A slow, clearly framed turn on a model | Actual sample, recorded sample size, model measurements |
| How does this fastening work? | One uninterrupted opening and closing | Physical garment and functioning closure |
| How does the fabric move? | A restrained step or arm movement | Footage of the actual material under stated conditions |
| What fits in the bag? | Insert named everyday objects, then close it | Actual bag and objects of known dimensions |
| What does the back look like? | A stable rear view held long enough to inspect | Accurate rear reference; a still may be sufficient |

Pick one or two questions per clip. Combining a walking sequence, a fabric close-up, a packing demonstration, and a campaign montage makes approval harder and weakens the reason for watching.

## Choose filmed, generated, or hybrid production

**Film the sample when behavior is the claim.** Stretch, transparency under movement, compression, waterproofing, pocket capacity, and the mechanics of a closure need direct evidence. A generated sequence can depict plausible behavior without establishing that the sold product behaves that way. Do not use simulated drape as a fit test.

**Use AI motion for a reviewed presentation treatment.** A subtle camera move around an approved composition can extend the visual language of the catalog. Review every newly revealed surface against source photographs. If the source only shows the front, an invented back is a failed product asset even if the animation looks convincing.

**Use a hybrid edit when different shots have different jobs.** For example, open on a reviewed editorial frame, cut to filmed footage of a closure, and end on an accurate product still. Keep the demonstration itself intact: changing a background must not change an edge, a fabric property, or the point where a hand touches the garment.

For the source stills, Astria's [ecommerce workflow](https://www.astria.ai/ecommerce) describes product-photo inputs and reusable creative templates. Approve the garment in those outputs before considering animation. Product acceptance remains a production responsibility; access to a generation tool is not proof of accuracy.

## A practical 12-second jacket brief

This is an example production target, not a platform requirement. The question is: “How does this jacket sit from the side, and how does it fasten?” Use the same real jacket and model throughout the demonstration.

| Time | Shot | Approval criterion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 seconds | Front view, neutral stance, jacket fully visible | Correct color and sample; hem and sleeves stay inside frame |
| 3–7 seconds | Model turns slowly to a three-quarter side view | Shoulder, sleeve, and side silhouette remain inspectable |
| 7–10 seconds | Close-up of fastening being closed | Real mechanism visible; hand does not obscure the decisive action |
| 10–12 seconds | Hold the finished front view | Closure state and garment shape match the preceding shot |

Record longer takes than the finished windows require, then select stable sections. Keep light, camera height, focal length, and white balance consistent. Lock exposure where an automatic adjustment would change the apparent color between views. Capture a clean still for the poster image during the same setup.

If you need to explain the sample size, place it in readable edited text or the adjacent product information. Keep essential detail away from player controls. Music is optional; the demonstration should make sense muted. Caption any spoken explanation and retain the caption text in the delivery package.

## Review motion against the SKU, frame by frame

An accurate first frame does not approve the rest of a generated clip. Play the entire export at normal speed, then scrub the parts where the model turns, the camera reveals a new surface, or hands overlap the product. Compare those frames with front, back, and detail photographs.

Check four things separately:

- **Identity:** correct SKU, colorway, print placement, and included accessories.
- **Construction:** stable seams, button count, pocket position, hem, fastenings, and hardware.
- **Movement:** no shifting logos, melting edges, sudden length changes, or implausible contact between hand and cloth.
- **Meaning:** the sequence does not imply an unverified material property, fit result, or capacity.

The merchandising owner approves product truth; the creative owner approves presentation. A color error is not a styling preference. If a generated shot repeatedly changes a defining feature, replace that shot with filmed footage or an accurate still. Shortening it until the error is hard to notice does not solve the product problem.

The [consistent AI fashion models guide](./consistent-ai-fashion-models.md) addresses recurring casting. It does not replace the garment checks needed for each SKU.

## Deliver a product asset, not just an MP4

Hand off the final clip with its poster, caption text or track when needed, SKU and colorway, intended gallery position, sample information, approval owner, and source references. Give the files explicit versions, such as `JK104_charcoal_closure_v03.mp4` and `JK104_charcoal_closure_poster_v03.jpg`.

Choose the aspect ratio by testing the actual product viewer. A portrait garment does not automatically require a full-screen social crop. Keep the hem, sleeves, and demonstrated detail visible in both the main player and its thumbnail. Retain a high-quality master and create a delivery export that balances readable texture with mobile load time.

For Shopify, directly uploaded product videos currently accept MP4, MOV, or WebM, up to 10 minutes, 1 GB, and 4K. Those are upload ceilings. Shopify also requires a theme that supports video; satisfying the file limits alone does not validate the storefront experience. Verify the current [Shopify product media requirements](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-media-types) before delivery.

Gallery ordering, variant behavior, and theme integration belong in the [Shopify product media guide](./shopify-product-media-guide.md). For this production handoff, require a mobile preview showing the correct product, a useful poster, accessible playback controls, and no unexpected crop before signing off.

## Budget by approved clip and demonstrated action

A quote for “20 videos” hides most of the work. State how many SKUs, colorways, actions, ratios, and revisions are included. Separate sample preparation, capture or generation, edit, product review, captions, export, and storefront QA. Record rejected generations and retakes as production costs.

A useful calculation is:

**Cost per approved SKU clip = total pilot production cost ÷ approved SKU clips.**

For illustration only, a $900 pilot that delivers six approved SKU clips costs $150 per clip. Three aspect-ratio exports of each clip remain six underlying product demonstrations; count the exports separately when estimating delivery work. These figures are arithmetic examples, not vendor rates.

Record the reason for each rejection. If most expense comes from a complex fastening, film that action once per relevant sample setup. If it comes from mismatched colorways, improve the reference and approval process before increasing generation volume. The [cost per SKU guide](./product-photography-cost-per-sku.md) covers the wider catalog budget.

## Run a pilot that can justify the next batch

Choose a small product family with a repeatable question, adequate traffic, and stable stock. Keep photography, copy, price, promotions, and gallery order consistent except for the video being tested. Where tooling permits, randomly assign eligible PDP visitors to a gallery with or without that video.

Name one primary outcome before the test, such as purchase rate per eligible visitor. Track playback and completion as diagnostics, alongside add-to-cart, mobile loading, and later returns or support questions. Count the full assigned group, including visitors who never press play. People who choose to watch a product video may already be more interested in buying.

Set the required sample and decision window from the store's baseline traffic and the effect worth detecting. Do not prescribe a universal seven-day or hundred-view winner. If the pilot is inconclusive, record that result and the production cost; it does not justify rolling motion out to the whole catalog.

An approved PDP demonstration can also supply evidence for an ad. The [fashion video ads guide](./fashion-video-ads-guide.md) explains how to build and test acquisition edits without turning the product page into an advertising montage.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should an apparel product video be?

Start with the time needed to answer one buying question clearly. A 12-second jacket demonstration is a useful brief to test, not a universal optimum. Remove introductions and repeated angles before shortening the action the shopper needs to inspect.

### Can AI-generated video replace filmed garment demonstrations?

Use filmed evidence when the claim concerns actual stretch, drape, transparency, fit, capacity, or a working closure. Generated motion can support reviewed presentation shots, but plausible movement does not establish the physical behavior of the sold product.

### What should a clothing product-video handoff include?

Deliver the approved clip, a poster image, caption material where needed, SKU and colorway, sample information, source references, version, and approval owner. Validate the crop and playback in the actual mobile product viewer.

### How do you measure whether PDP video helps sales?

Compare eligible visitors assigned to a gallery with or without the video while holding other changes steady. Use a preselected commerce outcome and track loading and playback as diagnostics. Comparing watchers with non-watchers alone does not establish a causal effect.
