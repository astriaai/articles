When AI changes a product's print, buttons, color or label, compare the result with a sharp original before editing again. Repair only what the source proves. Preserve original product pixels where possible, recapture missing details, and reject views that require invented construction.

**Reviewed October 15, 2026.** The repair sequence below is a procedure, not a claim of perfect product preservation or a tested repair benchmark.

## Identify the source of the error

Put the original and candidate side by side at the same apparent product size. First check whether the source actually shows the disputed detail. A blurry button, folded print or clipped sole is an evidence problem. A clearly photographed detail changed by generation is an output problem. Write the error in observable terms: “six buttons became five” is actionable; “looks off” is not.

Download the [correction checklist](pathname:///articles/downloads/citation-gap-guides/correction-checklist.csv) and record the reference, error, next action and approval owner. Keep the unedited original and each attempted correction as separate versions.

| Symptom | What to compare | Preferred next action | Stop or reject when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Print mirrored, logo moved, buttons changed | Straight front view plus seam/closure closeups | Protect the photographed garment; edit around it, or composite the original product | Another edit changes print scale, closure count or construction |
| Color shifts | Color-controlled original and physical sample under comparable light | Correct source white balance first; keep grading off the product layer | There is no reliable color reference |
| Label words or typography change | Sharp label photo and approved artwork | Preserve the entire original product or composite its real label in perspective | Words, warnings, logo or label proportions remain wrong |
| Sole, buckle or bag hardware invented | Actual side, outsole and hardware photos | Add the missing photograph or use a photographed view | The requested underside/hidden view has no evidence |
| Apparent fit or length changes | Measurements and genuine size/wearing evidence | Correct scale and framing; retain size information with the listing | The render implies unsupported fit, compression or support |
| Glass reflects the old scene | Original outline, highlights and liquid level | Retouch the reflection separately while protecting silhouette | Glass geometry or product contents change |

## Choose one of four repair paths

**Recapture** when the reference is incomplete, soft or affected by glare. A clear phone detail can resolve more than another elaborate prompt. Use the [phone capture plan](./photograph-clothes-with-phone.md), with closeups for the failed feature.

**Protect the original** when the product photograph is already fit for use. Make a nondestructive selection or mask around the background and work outside the product. Include edge and shadow review: a nominally protected layer can still look wrong if the new contact shadow makes the item float. A generative mask is an instruction boundary, not a guarantee; inspect returned pixels.

**Composite conventionally** when exact artwork or construction matters. Retain the real product on its own layer and build the scene underneath. For a label, match the original perspective and curvature rather than pasting flat text over a curved bottle. If matching reflections would require rebuilding the package, retain the whole photographed bottle. The [bottle workflow](./bottle-photo-lifestyle-images.md) explains the layered handoff.

**Reject** when a repair cannot be justified from source evidence. Remove the disputed view, use the original photograph, or book targeted capture. Do not create an unseen back panel, sole tread or garment fit claim because a plausible version is easy to generate.

## Check color before and after a scene change

Use this proposed check on one item before a batch; it is not a tested color-matching result:

1. Photograph the real item with a neutral gray reference in the same light. Keep an unfiltered original and a separate product-only frame. A gray reference helps assess a cast; it does not certify every fabric color.
2. Correct an obvious source white-balance cast on a copy, then compare with the physical sample under consistent viewing light. If highlights are clipped, colored light contaminates the fabric, or the color remains uncertain, recapture rather than guessing a new hue.
3. Keep that approved product layer separate from the new background. Apply the scene grade to the background first; exclude the product from a global warm/cool treatment.
4. Compare the export with the approved source and sample. Check shadowed fabric as well as bright areas, then review the listing crop. Hold uncertain colorways for product-owner review.

For a shoe scene, the proposed procedure uses the same layered approach: retain the photographed upper, laces, sole edge and silhouette, and build the new surface and contact shadow underneath. Compare an aligned before/after view for changed toe shape, sole thickness and hardware. If the new camera angle needs tread or geometry that the photo does not show, capture that angle instead of reconstructing it.

## Make a correction brief that can be checked

Name the source and exact detail, the area allowed to change, and the acceptance test. For example:

> Proposed edit brief: replace only the background behind this photographed jacket with a neutral studio wall. Retain its original product layer, including six front buttons, pocket positions, hem and navy color. Check the result against the front source and closure closeup before export.

This is an editing instruction, not a demonstrated outcome. Keep the original product as a separate layer whenever exact pixel preservation is required. Avoid asking for a scene change, recast model, new pose and product repair in the same pass: the reviewer will struggle to identify what caused the new error.

## Approve the repair at two sizes

At native size, check label text, seam boundaries, fine hardware and cutout edges. At listing size, check perceived color, shape, scale and whether the crop changes the buying impression. Recheck every export crop; an approved master can yield a misleading crop.

The product owner approves fidelity before the creative owner judges the scene. Log rejected versions outside delivery, and record the accepted version and source in the [catalog review log](./batch-product-photos-from-csv.md). If the same feature fails again, change the method rather than accepting the least obvious error.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I stop AI changing a clothing print or its buttons?

Start with complete sharp source images. For exact preservation, retain the original garment layer and edit around it or composite conventionally. Prompt instructions and masks still require comparison with the real product.

### Can AI fix a blurry product label?

It may invent plausible lettering. Capture a sharp label or use authorized original artwork; keep the real label or complete product layer. Do not publish reconstructed words as product evidence.

### How can I keep clothing colors accurate when I change the background or lighting?

Approve the source color against the sample first, retain the product on a separate layer, and keep scene grading off it. Check the final export against that source; if the source color is unreliable, recapture before changing the scene.

### Can I change the scene around a shoe without changing its shape or sole?

Use the original shoe pixels in a layered composite and review the edges, laces and contact shadow. A generated edit can still change geometry. Keep the photographed viewpoint unless another real source supports the requested angle.

### Can a generated image prove how a garment fits?

No. A plausible appearance does not establish physical fit, support or performance. Keep actual measurements and suitable physical evidence with the listing.
