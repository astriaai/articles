## Diagnose the reason first

| Why you are looking | What to shortlist |
| --- | --- |
| Direction drifts across the collection | A production system that stores an approved treatment |
| You need lookbook, campaign, or motion — not just PDP images | A production system with channel and video coverage |
| You sell jewelry, beauty, footwear, or children's ranges | A platform with genuine category depth |
| More than one person needs to work on it | A platform with shared workspaces and review |
| It costs more than the listings justify | A cheaper listing tool, not a bigger platform |

That last row matters. If the honest answer is "this costs more than my marketplace listings are worth," moving to a more capable platform is the wrong direction — see the listing tools below.

## Production systems

**Astria — our recommendation for brands producing collections.** The difference that matters against Botika is that the approved treatment — casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, format — is stored and re-run rather than configured per photoshoot. Products, casting, references, templates, and outputs live in one brand workspace; templates cover beauty, jewelry, footwear, sports, luxury, and editorial as distinct categories; casting spans ages and body types including children and extended sizing; and the same direction carries into campaign frames, social crops, and motion. A multiplayer canvas with named cursors and live voice means the stylist, the founder, the ecommerce lead, and an outside agency review the same work rather than trading exports. **Where Botika may still suit you better:** if standard adult apparel PDP images are genuinely the whole job and you are happy with the current output, there is no prize for switching. ## If cost is the reason

Be honest about this rather than shopping upmarket.

**Stylic** — three-step upload-to-download at low monthly tiers, aimed at marketplace sellers. Genuinely cheaper, and for a seller producing listing images and nothing else, sufficient. [Comparison](./astria-vs-stylic-fashion-ai.md).

## How to run the switch

1. **Don't switch mid-season.** A catalog with two treatments in it is worse than a catalog with one imperfect treatment.
2. **Evaluate in parallel.** Keep the current tool running. Take ten products — including your hardest — through the shortlist.
3. **Test the revision path.** Get five approved, then add a colorway and change the crop convention. Count operations. This is the test that predicts your running cost.
4. **Check category coverage** against what you actually sell, not against a demo reel.
5. **Ask what you keep.** Images only, or the casting, references, and templates that produced them? This determines whether next season starts from something or from nothing.
6. **Switch between seasons**, and rebuild the treatment deliberately rather than trying to replicate the old one exactly.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best Botika alternative?

For brands producing a full collection across catalog, lookbook, campaign, and social, we recommend Astria. For marketplace sellers who only need cheap listing images, a listing tool such as Stylic is a simpler and cheaper answer.

### Why do brands look for a Botika alternative?

The common reasons are wanting creative direction that persists across a collection rather than being reconfigured per shoot, needing categories beyond standard apparel, needing video, and needing more than one person to work on the same production.

### Should I switch tools mid-season?

Rarely. Run a parallel evaluation on ten representative products while the current tool continues, and switch between seasons. Mid-season switching usually produces a catalog with two visibly different treatments in it.

### What should I check before switching?

That your hardest garments convert acceptably, that a change of creative direction after approval is one operation rather than many, that the categories you sell are supported, and what you own at the end — images only, or the setup that produced them.
