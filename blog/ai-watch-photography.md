**A watch campaign needs a convincing photograph and a convincing watch.** The setting can change from a quiet workbench to a theatre, but the maker's name, dial layout and hand shapes still need to belong to the reference product. Tiny lettering makes that a demanding test.

We created two reference-driven photographs for each of four German watch models, giving each pair its own visual direction. This article shares the eight prompts, a selection of finished images and close-ups of the small inscriptions. The shoot used **Nano Banana 2 at the 4K setting, with Astria Lynx enabled**.

These are independent AI-generated editorial studies, not commissioned campaigns or endorsements by the watchmakers. The scenes and wrists are generated; the watches were supplied as image references. Finished images are creative examples, not substitutes for verified product photographs.

## Four watches, four photography directions

The creative brief gave each watch a different palette, material setting and lighting treatment. The two prompts within a pair share that direction while changing the composition.

## Set up a reference-driven watch shoot

Start with a sharp photograph of the exact dial, case and strap you want to show. A clear front view gives the model evidence for the lettering, hands and indices. Add an oblique view when the case depth, crown or lugs are important. Use source photographs you have permission to use.

In [Astria's composer](https://www.astria.ai/prompts), attach the watch reference, choose Nano Banana 2, select a 4:3 composition and the 4K resolution setting, then open the cog wheel and enable **Lynx**. Generate one image for each prompt so each result has a clear brief to review. See the [Lynx guide](/lynx-product-details) for the reference-detail workflow.

All eight prompts below were used for this shoot. We have replaced their workspace-specific reference tokens with “the reference watch” so they can be reused with your own attached image. Instructions such as “preserve exactly” express the target; they do not guarantee the result.

## Review the watch at two scales

At page size, check that the image works as a photograph: the watch should be the subject, the materials should look credible and the lighting should fit the intended campaign. Then inspect a close crop against the exact source.

| Check | What to compare |
| --- | --- |
| Tiny text | Every character, punctuation mark, line break and curved inscription |
| Hands | Shape, length, openings, color and position, including the small-seconds hand |
| Dial | Numerals, minute markers, subdial placement, plaque size and finish |
| Case and strap | Crown, lugs, attachment points, stitching and material pattern |
| Visibility | Sleeves, glare or props covering identifying details |

Lynx can help refine small reference details, but the review still has to cover the whole product. A clear logo does not prove that a seconds hand, marker or strap is faithful. When a detail cannot be checked in the reference, add a sharper source before treating the generated result as product evidence.

## How these examples were made

The shoot ran on November 1, 2026 in Astria's Articles workspace. Each of the four references was used for two prompts, with one image requested per prompt. All eight used Nano Banana 2, a 4:3 request, the 4K setting and Lynx enabled. Finished files were returned at 6,944 × 5,185 pixels; the full-scene web copies shown here are reduced to 2,400 pixels wide.

The close-ups are crops of the finished files, not separate generations. No lettering was manually repainted, and no detail crop was upscaled. These selected results demonstrate a creative workflow; they are not a controlled before-and-after measurement of Lynx. For matched before and finished frames, see the [Lynx examples](/lynx-product-details).
