Before uploading product photos to an AI service, confirm that you can authorize processing and the intended commercial use. Check supplier-image rights, any person's likeness, the selected provider's data terms and the final image's claims. Permission to sell a product does not itself answer all four questions.

## Keep the permissions separate

Download the [provider and rights questionnaire](pathname:///articles/downloads/citation-gap-guides/provider-questionnaire.txt). Assign an owner and dated source or contract clause to every answer. Use OPEN for unresolved questions; do not convert “not found” into “permitted.”

| Layer | Evidence to collect | Gate before upload or publication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Source photograph | Creator, owner, supplier license and permitted edits/processing | Written permission covers the intended workflow and channels |
| Likeness | Appropriate permission for the person and proposed synthetic use | Intended endorsement, territory, duration and context are addressed |
| Data processing | Provider/model route, retention, training, access and transfer terms | Applicable privacy and contractual obligations are resolved |
| Output use | Commercial-use terms, third-party material and human contributions | Contractual permission and legal rights are not confused |
| Product representation | Actual SKU evidence, label approval and channel rules | Final image does not invent what is being sold |

A supplier may authorize listing a photograph without authorizing broad AI processing, sublicensing or advertising edits. Ask for the actual license and proposed use. If no reliable permission record exists, request new photographs or hold those materials from upload. A product-only photograph can also contain personal data in reflections, backgrounds, labels or metadata.

## Ask which jurisdiction and use are involved

For a US copyright question, the [US Copyright Office's 2025 report announcement](https://www.copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html) explains that copyrightability depends on sufficient human authorship; merely supplying prompts does not establish that authorship. Contractual ownership language therefore does not itself guarantee copyright protection or exclusivity. Keep records of original photography, selection, compositing and other human creative contributions.

For a workflow within GDPR's scope, the [European Data Protection Board's small-business guide](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/learn-the-basics/data-protection-basics_en) identifies photos, video and audio of identifiable people as examples of personal data. Assess roles, lawful basis, notices, processing terms and any transfers for the actual project. This is separate from whether a model release permits a particular commercial campaign.

Other markets can apply different privacy, likeness, advertising and disclosure rules. Record where the content will run and who will check those rules; do not assume that one release or an “AI generated” caption resolves every obligation. If the image implies a product capability, size, ingredient or endorsement, obtain the appropriate factual and rights review before release.

## Make the provider answer the operational questions

Ask which selected model and subprocessors receive inputs, where copies live, what training permission applies, who can access private materials, and what deletion removes. Distinguish original files, outputs, operational logs and backups. Ask how shared workspaces, public links and connected apps affect visibility and copies.

Record the exact service/model and dated agreement rather than relying on a general vendor badge. If a required answer remains open, use a nonsensitive, rights-cleared limited pilot only where your reviewer authorizes it; do not upload the disputed materials while waiting.

## Attach a release record to the images

Save source permissions, likeness permissions where relevant, approved product references, selected provider terms, review decisions and the accepted file list. The release owner signs the permitted markets/channels and any exclusions. Recheck when a provider, model, agreement, likeness, campaign context or intended market changes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I make on-model photos from supplier images for my online boutique?

Only after checking the actual permission for editing, AI processing and intended commercial use. Being authorized to sell the product does not establish the scope of the photograph's license.

### What should I ask an AI creative production provider before trusting them with my brand's images?

Ask about source and likeness rights, selected model providers, training, confidentiality, visibility, retention, deletion, connected-app copies, applicable agreements and output-use conditions. Save dated evidence, assign an owner and hold unresolved materials from upload.

### Can I use the same model across a whole collection without booking them for every shoot?

For a real person's likeness, check whether the agreement covers synthetic reuse, the collection, channels, territory and duration. Hold new uses until your reviewer confirms that scope. Astria's [terms](https://www.astria.ai/terms) require consent for submitted images of people; a reusable identity reference is not itself a release. The [reference-role walkthrough](./consistent-ai-fashion-models.md) covers the production setup separately.

### What should I check before using AI model photos on my clothing product pages?

Resolve source and likeness permissions, provider/data conditions and the intended publishing use. Then compare each image with the actual SKU, including color, print, closures, size and fit implications. Save the approved files and release decision; hold any image with unresolved rights or product evidence.

### Does Astria promise never to train on uploaded images?

Its current public terms include limited training and evaluation permission, subject to their stated restrictions, provider conditions, applicable agreements and law. Review the current terms and resolve your project's requirements before upload.

### Do I automatically own copyright in every AI output?

Contractual commercial-use rights and copyright protection are separate questions. Applicable law and human creative contributions matter; the US Copyright Office does not treat prompts alone as sufficient authorship.
