## WearView pricing: plans, resolution, and credits

Base allowances displayed on the [pricing page](https://www.wearview.co/pricing), September 5, 2026:

| Plan | Monthly billing, USD | Credits/month |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Lite | $29 | 50 |
| Pro | $49 | 200 |
| Advanced | $99 | 500 |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom |

Yearly billing displays lower monthly equivalents; those are not the month-to-month prices. The credit selectors may offer other volumes.

| Operation | Listed credits |
| --- | ---: |
| Virtual try-on, HD | 1/image |
| General image, HD | 2/image |
| Image, 2K | 3/image |
| Image, 4K | 5/image |
| Video, 720p | 10/video |
| Video, 1080p | 20/video |

Advanced explicitly includes rollover. The page says top-up packs do not expire and lists API access under Enterprise. Confirm the rollover conditions for your chosen plan. These are generation costs, not guarantees of approved output.

### A collection budget at 4K

For an illustrative 60-style brief with four generated 4K images per style: 60 × 4 × 5 = **1,200 credits**. That is 700 beyond the displayed Advanced base allowance, before retries or video. Price additional capacity for the production month; the base allowance is not a hard limit on what can be purchased.

Do the same calculation using the resolution you actually need. Inspect a detail crop before paying for 4K across the entire catalog, and do not assume a larger file restores product details that were generated incorrectly. Turn the pilot into a [cost-per-SKU budget](./product-photography-cost-per-sku.md).

## WearView review: more than a product-page tool

WearView's [homepage](https://www.wearview.co/) lists virtual try-on, product-to-model, consistent models, pose control, ghost mannequin, model swap, and video. It explicitly presents lookbook and brand-campaign use cases. Include those outputs when comparing it with Astria.

Our assessment: this is a relevant shortlist for an apparel team whose first task is putting real garments on models. A broader use-case list still needs testing. A lookbook requires a sequence and a consistent point of view; a set of attractive pose variations does not establish either.

For the image-production requirements, use the [AI lookbook guide](./ai-lookbook-guide.md). For the finished document, see [how to create a lookbook](./how-to-create-a-lookbook.md).

## Astria: another route to the collection brief

Astria publishes [fashion workflows](https://www.astria.ai/ecommerce) built around product photos and templates. Its [lookbook gallery](https://www.astria.ai/gallery/workspaces) provides collection examples to inspect.

Give both tools the same hero reference and ask for the same deliverables. Keep product accuracy separate from aesthetic preference: a frame can win the mood review and fail the garment check. The pilot should show whether the team can preserve both across a collection.

## A realistic workflow comparison: the week the campaign brief lands

Start with five representative garments and produce the required product views. Approve those against the references, then ask for a campaign frame with different lighting and a wider composition. Keep the casting and garment fixed while the scene changes.

This is the revealing test. It exposes whether creative direction can change without altering a hem, fastening, print scale, or face. Repeat with a layered look and then a new colourway. Record the rejected attempts and any manual correction; do not count only the successful final click.

Have the person who normally reviews your brand's imagery approve the results without platform labels. Then give the saved work to a colleague and ask them to extend it. A repeatable workflow should survive that handoff. Read [consistent AI fashion models](./consistent-ai-fashion-models.md) for a focused casting test.

## Choose WearView if...

- Garment-to-model work is the first requirement you want to test.
- You can budget the actual operation and resolution mix against its plans.
- Its product, lookbook, and campaign outputs pass the same garment review.

## Choose Astria if...

- You want to begin with a fashion template and adapt it to approved products.
- The evaluation centres on the whole lookbook or campaign treatment.
- Its pilot produces the required consistency with acceptable review effort.

## What to test before choosing either platform

Use an identical output list and deadline. Compare product fidelity, consistency across images, correction time, and accepted output cost. Neither the headline subscription price nor a vendor's generation-speed claim measures the time to an approved collection.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does WearView produce lookbook and campaign images?

Yes. WearView explicitly advertises both uses. Test a complete set for garment fidelity, casting, and creative direction rather than assuming the tool is limited to product pages.

### Is every WearView image one credit?

No. The one-credit rate is for HD virtual try-on. Other image operations and higher resolutions have different listed rates; use the pricing table for the intended workflow.

### Is Astria cheaper than WearView?

This review does not establish that. Price identical approved deliverables, including additional attempts, corrections, human review, and any unused subscription capacity.
