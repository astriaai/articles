## Check the bottle before planning the scene

Capture the complete bottle on a simple background with enough contrast to see the glass, cap and base. Use diffuse light and inspect reflections: a bright stripe across the label can destroy the words you need to preserve. Move lights or the bottle until essential text is readable without changing the intended viewing angle.

Photograph the label close enough to resolve small type, plus the cap, closure and any distinctive embossing. Retain approved artwork where available. Add side/back photographs only when those views belong in the deliverable. A front label photo cannot establish rear text, bottle depth, contents or an unseen dispenser mechanism.

| Input | Readiness check | Hold when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whole bottle photograph | Sharp contour, complete cap/base, usable reflections | Edges disappear or the bottle is clipped |
| Label detail | Correct words, punctuation, logo and fine print readable | Glare, blur or compression hides text |
| Product specification | Volume, color, cap type, fill level and version identified | The sample and sold product differ |
| Rights record | Photograph and artwork cleared for intended processing/use | Supplier permission is unclear |

Preserve originals at full resolution. More resolution cannot recover words that were never captured. The [phone capture checks](./photograph-clothes-with-phone.md) also apply to focus, original files and stable diffuse lighting, but reflective packaging may need specialist capture.

## Plan a scene the photographed angle can support

Choose a surface, background, light direction and camera relation that work with the existing bottle. A dry neutral plinth is easier to reconcile with a clean original than an underwater view or a dramatic rotated bottle. Do not request a new angle unless your sources support it.

Check the implied claims. Fruit, ingredients, medical props or athletic settings can suggest product composition or effects. Use only approved, accurate associations. Keep any campaign wording in a separate layout layer and pass it through the normal claims review.

Write down invariants: bottle contour, cap, label position and typography, contents/fill level, transparency and product color. Those are approval criteria rather than a promise that a model will preserve them.

## Preserve the original product in layers

1. Keep a locked original photograph. Duplicate it for nondestructive masking and retouch; retain the full bottle rather than regenerating its label.
2. Build or generate the background separately. If using generation, supply cleared reference imagery for the intended scene and review its perspective and light direction. This guide supplies no generated background or test outcome.
3. Composite the real bottle over the scene. Refine edge transparency, cap edges and contact shadow at native size. A transparent bottle can reveal its old background; do not solve that by silently changing the glass shape or liquid color.
4. Retouch incompatible reflections on separate layers. If that requires reconstructing large portions of the bottle, change the scene or recapture under suitable light.
5. If using a separate label detail, match perspective, curvature, highlights and scale. When that cannot be done faithfully, keep the entire original product instead of pasting invented lettering.

A source-preserving composite still needs review. Halos, old background fragments and mismatched shadows can make a correct product look physically implausible. Ask a retoucher to resolve those issues when the packaging is central to the sale.

## Approve typography before atmosphere

At native size, read the entire visible label against the original or authorized artwork. Check capitalization, quantities, claims, symbols, spacing and logo proportions. Compare cap, closure, embossing and silhouette. Then inspect the ad/listing size: the bottle should remain the correct apparent color and shape with natural grounding.

**Stop if the label is wrong or unverifiable.** Do not select the most attractive of several inaccurate labels. Use the original photograph, change the composition or recapture. The [repair checklist](./fix-ai-product-photo-errors.md) routes repeated label failures to original-preserving methods.

Export only approved scene/crop versions, with SKU, package version, source record and reviewer in the [accepted-output manifest](./batch-product-photos-from-csv.md). A small social crop can remove required information even when the master passed, so inspect each export separately.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I make beauty product photos without the label text getting messed up?

A reference-led model may still alter lettering or geometry. For exact preservation, keep the original bottle photograph as a separate layer and build the scene around it, then check the composite at native size.

### Can I repair the label by asking for the correct words again?

Do not rely on generated lettering as product evidence. Use a sharp original label or authorized artwork with appropriate perspective, or retain the complete photographed bottle.

### How do I get polished product images for a supplement brand when I only have bottle photos?

Check that the bottle, cap and label are sharp and current, then retain the original bottle layer in a scene compatible with its photographed angle. Review exact label words and approved claims, reflections, edges and every export crop. Recapture or use specialist retouch if the source cannot support the scene.

### How can I create lifestyle photos for skincare products without building a studio set?

Start with a sharp, readable bottle photograph and design a scene with compatible perspective and lighting. Composite the original product over the new background, preserving the label, cap and glass edges. Stop for unrecoverable glare or incompatible transparency; a different scene or new bottle capture may be necessary.
