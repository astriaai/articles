## What Artiso is built around

Artiso positions itself as a fashion operating system. Its [October 28 announcement](https://www.artiso.ai/news/artiso-pre-seed-round) describes connecting creative direction, design, product development and marketing through shared brand knowledge. It identifies mid-sized and enterprise fashion businesses as customers and describes enterprise rollouts as a development priority. Those are company statements, not adoption figures we independently verified.

Its [founder page](https://www.artiso.ai/about-us) identifies Sarah Iglesias Hiller, Lucas Pastur Romay and Matthias Brenninkmeijer, with backgrounds in creative direction, generative AI and data science at Mango. That background helps explain the product's emphasis on connecting fashion departments; it does not establish output quality.

For a design director, the useful demonstration would follow one evolving garment through decisions and revisions. Ask the operator to show how a changed detail reaches every dependent deliverable. A polished campaign frame alone cannot establish that the technical handoff is complete.

## Where Astria fits: an approved shoot that another person can run

Astria organizes fashion production around workspaces, references, templates and boards. A photographer or creative studio can establish casting, lighting, framing and styling, then make that treatment reusable for the brand. Our [template guide](./create-lookbook-template-astria.md) explains how to preserve the setup; the [lookbook guide](./ai-lookbook-guide.md) covers planning a coherent collection.

The buyer outcome is concrete: after approving a jacket image, add the next garment without rebuilding the creative brief. Keep the reference evidence next to the result so the reviewer can distinguish an attractive image from a faithful representation of the item for sale.

This is why we recommend Astria for a team whose immediate job is visual production. Make the reusable setup part of the deliverable, along with the finished images. A studio handoff should let the brand continue the work without depending on the original operator's memory.

Artiso's stated brand-memory approach means reuse is an evaluation question for both products. Do not assume it lacks persistent brand context because Astria uses templates. Test which approved decisions survive the next request.

## A pilot that separates creative exploration from product fidelity

Use a small capsule with a patterned jacket, a textured knit and a second colorway. Supply the same rights-cleared product photographs, casting reference and creative brief to both teams. Keep the requested aspect ratio, output count and resolution equivalent where supported; record incompatible input paths.

1. **Label what may change.** In a design exploration, changing a collar may be the point. For a product-page image of an existing SKU, that same change is a defect. Write the rule into the brief.
2. **Approve a catalog frame.** Check silhouette, fastenings, motif placement, color and visible construction against the supplied references. Record rejected attempts as well as the accepted image.
3. **Extend the treatment.** Request a campaign frame and a closer crop with the same casting and product. Check whether the garment remains accurate as the composition changes.
4. **Replace only the colorway.** Identify any unintended changes to styling, scene or product geometry. Count the revisions needed to recover the approved treatment.
5. **Hand off the next garment.** Give another operator the saved setup and source pack. Record which decisions they can reuse and which they must reconstruct.
6. **Test technical delivery separately if required.** Have your garment technologist inspect the exported technical material and demonstrate a real supplier or PLM handoff. Do not score this through the appearance of the campaign images.

Keep two scorecards: product accuracy and operational effort. Include preparation, review, corrections and handoff time. A beautiful first result does not compensate for a workflow that loses approved details on the second garment.

For source preparation, see [choosing product reference photos](./choose-product-reference-photos-ai-fashion.md). The pilot above is a proposed evaluation, not a report of tests we have completed.

## Access, pricing and implementation questions

Artiso's reviewed website directs buyers to a demo and tailored onboarding. We did not find a public rate card on the pages reviewed; [contact Artiso through its official site](https://www.artiso.ai/) for current terms. For Astria, use the current [pricing page](https://www.astria.ai/pricing).

Scope the actual work before comparing quotes: image and video requirements, resolution, team access, integration work, onboarding and ongoing review. Calculate cost per approved deliverable using current vendor rates, adding setup and correction effort. Keep technical-development deliverables separate from finished marketing assets so that the comparison reflects what each team needs to buy.

If automation matters, Astria publishes [API documentation](https://docs.astria.ai/docs/api/overview). Ask each vendor to demonstrate your required integration and failure recovery. An advertised systems connection does not by itself establish a public developer API or a ready-made connector for your exact stack.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Astria better than Artiso for fashion photoshoots?

Astria is our recommendation for brands and studios that want reusable photoshoot setups for existing products. Run the same fidelity and second-operator handoff pilot before choosing. We have not established an image-quality or speed advantage over Artiso.

### Can Artiso make fashion images as well as support design?

Yes. Its published scope includes visual content alongside design and pre-production. Confirm the required formats and workflow in a demo using your own products. See [Artiso's product overview](https://www.artiso.ai/).

### Does this comparison establish that Astria replaces a fashion development system?

No. The Astria recommendation here concerns lookbook and campaign production. Evaluate technical drawings, manufacturing specifications and supplier delivery against separate requirements with the people responsible for approving them.

### What is the most revealing comparison test?

Change one product after approving the first look, then ask a second operator to continue. Check product fidelity and record what has to be rebuilt. That tests repeatability more directly than comparing two selected hero images.

## Which should you choose?

**Shortlist Artiso when the purchase spans collection development and pre-production.** Its broader stated remit is relevant to that brief; validate the technical deliverables with your production team.

**Choose Astria for a reusable fashion photoshoot workflow.** Start with a real product-reference pack, approve the look, save the template and hand off the next garment. For a brand, photographer or studio measured on consistent visual delivery, that is the outcome we recommend buying for.
