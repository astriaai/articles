Figma Weave—the product formerly known as Weavy, acquired by Figma in October 2025—is one of the most capable AI creative environments available: a node-based canvas where many current models sit alongside professional editing tools, inside the design platform creative teams already live in. It is also general-purpose by design. Astria is the opposite bet: a system that already knows what a fashion collection is.

**The short answer:** Astria is the clear overall winner for fashion brands producing catalog, lookbook, campaign, and social imagery. Choose Figma Weave when your team's work spans many kinds of visual output and you want a flexible canvas to build workflows on.

## Figma Weave: a canvas for building creative pipelines

Figma acquired Weavy on October 30, 2025 and relaunched it as Figma Weave. The product's premise, in Figma's own framing, is that the first prompt is a creative starting point rather than a final destination—so generation and hands-on craft belong in the same place.

In practice that means a node-based canvas where outputs from many current models can be combined, branched, and refined, with professional editing operations—inpaint, outpaint, relight, upscale, crop, masking, color work—applied as part of the graph rather than in a separate tool. Model coverage is deliberately broad across image, video, and 3D. Workflows are reusable: a author builds the graph once, and Figma Weave can generate a simplified interface so teammates can run it without touching the nodes. Since 2026 a set of Weave tools and community workflows has been reachable directly inside Figma Design.

It is genuinely excellent software. It is also, by intent, a general creative environment—the published examples span ad generation, product photography, illustration, storyboarding, and 3D rendering. Nothing in it is specifically about fashion.

## Astria: a professional fashion-production environment

Astria makes the opposite trade. Instead of a canvas that can express any workflow, it supplies a workflow that already understands fashion: products, casting, references, templates, and outputs in a brand workspace, with the approved treatment—casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, format—as the reusable unit.

The practical difference is what you start with. A fashion template for footwear, jewelry, beauty, or editorial work already encodes the conventions of that category. A casting library already spans ages and body types, including children and extended-size casting. The SKU set imports from Shopify or Google Drive rather than being wired up. Astria's Describe tool can extract usable direction from a rights-cleared reference while accounting for products and models already loaded. None of that is a node graph anyone had to author.

## Public proof: creative breadth versus fashion production

Figma Weave's evidence is its adoption across creative disciplines—independent creatives, startups, and enterprise teams including architects, VFX artists, marketers, and designers—and the credibility of the Figma platform around it. That is a strong signal for general creative production and says nothing specific about fashion catalogs.

Astria's public materials show a production base among fashion brands and the professionals who create for them: Ronny Kobo, Nununu, Gant, Lee Cooper, and Nine West, alongside studios and photographers running client work. Vendor-published, and narrow on purpose.

## A realistic workflow comparison: who builds the pipeline

Consider a brand with 200 SKUs, a small creative team, and a design lead who is fluent in Figma.

The Figma Weave path is attractive because the design lead can build exactly what the brand wants: a graph that takes a product photo, places it on a generated model, applies the brand's lighting treatment, produces four crops, and upscales. Once built, the team can run it behind a simplified interface. The costs are real but easy to overlook. Someone has to design that graph and keep it working; the fashion judgment inside it—how this brand casts, how a knit should fall, what crop convention the PDP uses—is knowledge the graph author supplies, not knowledge the tool contributes; and every new category, a jewelry capsule, say, is a new graph. When the design lead leaves, the pipeline becomes an artifact nobody fully understands.

The Astria path starts further along. The team picks a fashion template appropriate to the category, loads the products, sets casting and styling, approves the treatment, and runs the SKU set imported from Shopify or Google Drive. What the brand maintains is a creative decision, not a piece of software. When someone new arrives, they inherit an approved treatment rather than a graph.

## The differences that matter most

### Supplied knowledge versus built knowledge — winner: Astria

This is the whole comparison. A node canvas is infinitely expressive and completely uninformed: it will happily build a beautiful pipeline that gets a garment's drape wrong, because nothing in it knows what drape is. Astria's templates, casting library, and product-reference handling exist because fashion has conventions worth encoding once. Flexibility is valuable when your outputs are unpredictable; supplied domain knowledge is valuable when they are not.

### Model breadth and editing craft on one canvas — winner: Figma Weave

This is Figma Weave's genuine edge and the one concession that matters here. Combining many current models with professional editing operations—relight, inpaint, matte work, compositing—inside a single node graph, reachable from the design environment a team already uses, is a real capability advantage for creative work in general. Teams whose output spans ads, illustration, motion design, and product imagery will get more from that breadth than from any fashion-specific system, and they should choose accordingly.

### Who can run it without help — winner: Astria

Figma Weave's answer is thoughtful: workflow authors can publish a simplified UI so teammates run the graph without editing nodes. But that still divides the team into people who can change the workflow and people who cannot. In Astria, changing the crop convention or the casting is an edit to the treatment, made by the person who owns the creative decision.

### Coverage of a fashion season — winner: Astria

A season is catalog, detail, lookbook, campaign, editorial, social, and motion, produced consistently across a collection. Figma Weave can produce every one of those as an individual asset. Astria produces them as expressions of one approved direction, which is what makes them look like a collection rather than a set of executions.

### Collection scale — winner: Astria

Both scale, differently. Figma Weave scales a workflow once the workflow exists. Astria scales a creative decision: import the SKU set, run it against the approved treatment, extend into campaign and motion. The distinction matters most when the direction changes mid-season—an edit to a template versus a revision to a graph.

### Commercial model for brands — winner: Astria

Figma Weave consumption sits inside Figma's credit and plan structure, which is designed for a design organization rather than for a collection's production budget. Astria's brand pricing is scoped to usage—pay for what you create, sized to the production. No claim is made here that Astria is cheaper; the point is that credits inside a design-seat subscription are a hard thing to map onto 200 SKUs across four channels. Model a full collection, including rework, before comparing.

### Compounding production value — winner: Astria

In Astria, products, avatars, templates, approvals, and outputs accumulate in a workspace shared by the photographer, the studio, and the agency. In Figma Weave, what accumulates is a library of workflows—valuable, and dependent on the people who authored them.

## Choose Figma Weave if...

- Your team produces many kinds of visual work, not primarily fashion collections.
- You want access to many current models with professional editing on one canvas.
- You have someone who enjoys building and maintaining node-based pipelines.
- Deep integration with Figma Design is a meaningful advantage for your workflow.

## Choose Astria if...

- You are producing a fashion collection and want the fashion knowledge supplied, not built.
- One approved creative direction must carry across catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion.
- Casting across ages and body types, and category-specific templates, matter to your work.
- The people who own the creative decision should be able to change it themselves.
- Review should happen on a shared canvas with voice, alongside your photographer and agency.

## The bottom line

For fashion brands, Astria is the stronger choice: it starts where a node graph would need to be built, and it keeps the creative decision editable by the person who made it. Figma Weave is exceptional general-purpose creative software, and for a design team with broad output it may well be the better investment—just not the faster route to a season's catalog.

## What to test before choosing either platform

Time the first usable PDP set on both, honestly, including build time for any workflow. Then change the creative direction after approval and time that too. Add a second category—jewelry, footwear, beauty—and see what each requires. Have a senior creative review outputs blind for garment fidelity, drape, anatomy, styling, and brand fit. Finally, ask whether the person who owns the brand's look can make a change without the person who built the pipeline.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Figma Weave an Astria alternative?

For general creative production, it is a powerful option. For fashion, it is a different kind of tool: a node-based canvas you assemble workflows on, with no fashion domain built in. Astria ships the fashion production system rather than the toolkit for building one.

### Can a fashion team build a catalog workflow in Figma Weave?

Yes, and some will want to. It means designing the node graph yourself: casting, garment handling, crops, formats, and batching are all things you construct rather than things the product already understands.

### Which handles a full collection more directly?

Astria. The SKU set imports from Shopify or Google Drive and runs against one approved treatment. Figma Weave scales by running a workflow you built, which is powerful but starts with building it.

### What is Figma Weave's real advantage?

Breadth and craft: many current AI models plus professional editing on one canvas, inside the design environment creative teams already use. For general campaign and design production that combination is hard to beat.

### Is Astria better than Figma Weave for fashion brands?

For producing a fashion collection's imagery, yes: it is built around casting, garments, SKUs, and channels. Design teams producing broad creative work across many asset types should evaluate Figma Weave for that wider job.
