**Astria’s Mockup Lab applies an artwork file to a surface in an image.** Generate the photograph with a plain product surface, open the finished image in the lightbox, then select **Mockup**. Upload your print, select the object, and adjust its scale, position, wrapping and finish before saving or downloading.

For an apparel brand, that can mean placing a shirt graphic and its small caption on an on-model photograph. For a home-textile brand, it can mean applying the approved fabric repeat to a duvet cover and matching pillowcase while keeping the sheet plain. Those are the two lead examples below; packaging, a cup and a throw follow as secondary applications.

## Start with a shirt print and a bedding collection

These two examples show the files an apparel or bed-linen team needs to keep separate: the approved photograph, the print master, and the finished composite. ## Why separate the photograph from the print?

A product image has two different kinds of detail. The photograph establishes the garment cut or bedding construction, material, pose and lighting. The artwork establishes the exact lettering, symbol, illustration and repeat. Treating them separately lets you adjust the design without asking an image model to redraw the entire scene.

If a Nano Banana result has a misspelled small caption or an altered motif, supplying the artwork to Mockup Lab gives you direct control over that printed area. The compositor uses your image file as the print; you position and wrap it on the selected surface. It still resamples, shades and distorts that artwork to fit the photograph, so check the export. Very small letters can become unreadable even when the source file is correct.

Google’s [image-generation documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/image-generation) recommends a staged approach when generating text in images: prepare the wording before asking for the image. For existing brand typography, the workflow here goes further by bringing in the approved artwork itself. This is a production choice, not a claim that every Nano Banana output has the same failure.

For an ecommerce brand, the question is whether a customer can identify the actual variant. A convincing room image with the wrong duvet repeat is still the wrong product image. A shirt with almost-correct lettering may represent the wrong collection. Keep the design file and a photograph of the manufactured item available during review.

## How to open Mockup Lab after generating an image

The working path is:

**Generate → click the finished image → lightbox → Mockup → Add pattern → click the surface → adjust → Save to prompt or Download PNG.**

## Step 1: Generate a plain surface from your product reference

Open the [Astria composer](https://www.astria.ai/prompts), attach a sharp product reference and describe the photograph. If a person wears or holds the product, attach the appropriate person reference too. Ask for the target surface to be plain and unprinted while preserving the construction and material.

For an on-model shirt mockup, with the shirt and person reference attached:

> Using the supplied person and shirt as references, make a close ecommerce lookbook photograph from the head to just below the hips. Use a plain pocketless white cotton T-shirt; preserve the chosen product’s cut, dropped shoulder, ribbed neck and stitching. Relaxed three-quarter stance, arms clear of the shirt, white studio background, soft daylight. Keep the fabric plain white and unprinted, with gentle visible folds.

For a bedding mockup, with the bed-linen reference attached:

> Using the supplied bedding photograph as the reference, preserve the duvet cover’s dimensions, pillowcase, seams and fabric drape. Show the complete bed at a three-quarter angle in the same softly lit room. Keep the duvet cover and pillowcase solid ivory and unprinted, with broad gentle wrinkles. Keep the fitted sheet plain ivory and the duvet unobstructed.

These instructions deliberately remove the print during scene creation. Review the base before proceeding: an unwanted pocket, changed collar, wrong duvet drop or missing pillow seam will not be repaired by applying artwork.

Choose a view that exposes the area you need. A hand over a shirt graphic, a deep crease through a wordmark, or a tiny product in a wide room leaves little useful space for the print. Use a closer photograph when the design must be readable. Generate at a sufficiently high resolution for your intended crop; a larger export cannot recover information absent from the source.

## Step 2: Click the finished image to open the lightbox

Open the generated result itself. In its lightbox action panel, select **Mockup**. The keyboard shortcut is **M** while you are in the image viewer.

## Step 3: Choose Add pattern, then click the surface

Select the **plus button, Add pattern**, and choose your artwork image. For an isolated logo, use a PNG with a transparent background. For an all-over fabric design, use a seamless repeat tile. Flatten vector artwork to a sufficiently large raster image before uploading; the workflow shown here uses PNG files.

Once the artwork is ready, click the object in the photograph. The first selection may take longer while the selection model loads. Inspect the outline before judging the print.

Use **Mark** to refine the selection: click to include more of the object; **Shift-click** the background to exclude it. **Brush** adds to the selected area and **Eraser** removes from it. Keep the model’s skin, trousers, bed frame, fitted sheet and unprinted trims outside the selection. A print spilling onto a hand is a selection problem, not a problem with the artwork. Use a separate Add pattern layer for a matching pillowcase so it can have its own selection and placement.

## Step 4: Set the print size and placement in Fit

Open **Fit**. Switch **Tile artwork off** for one shirt graphic, logo or label. Leave it **on** for a repeating design. This distinction matters: a brand mark should not unexpectedly become an all-over print.

Adjust **Size** or **Tile size**, **Rotate**, **Move X** and **Move Y**. The **Move** tool also lets you drag the print; its frame supports resizing and rotation. Use the numerical controls when you need smaller adjustments.

For a repeat, match the manufactured product’s motif scale before deciding whether it looks attractive. If a motif measures 6 cm across a 60 cm visible span of fabric, roughly ten motifs should fit across that span before perspective and folds compress them. This is a visual check, not a physical measurement built into the editor. Use a sample photograph to verify it.

## Step 5: Follow the surface, then adjust the finish

Open **Surface** and keep **Wrap to shape** enabled to make the artwork follow the object. Begin with **Shape: Auto**. If the result misreads the surface, try **Rigid** for packaging, **Soft** for loose cloth, or **Draped** for broad surfaces with creases, such as a made bed or cushion.

Let shape processing finish before evaluating folds. If the app reports that shape reading failed, inspect the flat placement carefully; it is not evidence of a successful wrap. **Follow surface**, **Lens angle** and **Depth range** provide further adjustment, but start with selection and print scale. Increasing distortion cannot correct an incorrectly selected object.

Use **Selection edge** for a small outline correction. Shrinking pulls artwork inward; expanding extends it beyond the detected edge. Use the brush for a local mistake around a finger or seam instead of expanding the entire outline.

In **Finish**, choose the blend according to what you need:

| Control | Practical use |
| --- | --- |
| **Multiply** | Lets the existing surface show through the print. Useful on a light, plain substrate; the underlying color affects the result. |
| **Normal + Base colour** | Repaints the selected surface with a base color and places the artwork on it. Review seams, texture and shading after changing it. |
| **Fold lighting** | Adjusts how strongly the original shading influences the print. Check that folds remain visible without obscuring the design. |
| **Texture + Texture from: Photo** | Carries the photographed surface’s weave through the artwork. Useful for printed cotton. |
| **Texture from: Print** | Uses texture in the supplied artwork, such as a material scan. Choose this only when that source texture belongs in the result. |
| **Gloss / Opacity / Sharpness** | Adjusts the appearance of the composite. Excess gloss can make cotton look coated; low opacity can wash out brand colors. |

White artwork in Multiply will not behave like opaque white ink on dark cloth. Use a suitable blend and inspect against a physical sample. A finish control cannot establish the appearance of embroidery, foil, embossing or a particular printing process.

## Step 6: Review and export

Turn on **High-quality preview** for the final inspection. It renders the preview at the source photograph’s resolution; it does not add new source detail. Check the image at the intended storefront size and zoom into the artwork, selection edges and folds. Open the downloaded file too: an earlier preview may differ from the result after shape processing finishes.

Choose **Save to prompt** to attach the finished image to the generation, or **Download PNG** for a local file. The editor’s **Saved** status concerns the editable mockup settings; **Save to prompt** creates the finished image attachment. Wait for the save confirmation before leaving. Use **Back to image** to return to the lightbox.

## Fixing small details in Nano Banana product images

Choose the method according to the detail you have, rather than repeatedly rerunning the whole image.

| Situation | Useful next step |
| --- | --- |
| Approved logo or label artwork is available; the surface can be selected | Generate a plain surface and apply the file in Mockup Lab. Check print scale and perspective. |
| Only a product photograph exists; the detail is part of the photographed material | Use reference-based generation and consider [Lynx detail refinement](./lynx-product-details.md). Check the result against the reference. |
| Garment cut, box closure, cap or product proportions are wrong | Correct the base image or generate a better one before applying a print. |
| The text is simply too small in the final frame | Add a closer detail image. Correct wording alone cannot make subpixel letters readable. |
| Foil, embroidery or raised lettering determines the product’s appearance | Use photographs of the manufactured detail to verify the result. A flat artwork overlay does not prove that physical finish. |

Mockup Lab gives you manual placement and surface controls. [Lynx](./lynx-product-details.md) refines details from product references during generation. They address different inputs and levels of control; review the complete product whichever path you choose.

## Troubleshooting a product mockup

| What you see | What to check first |
| --- | --- |
| The logo repeats across the product | Turn **Tile artwork off** in Fit. |
| A white rectangle surrounds the logo | Export a transparent PNG instead of an artwork file with a baked-in background. |
| Print appears on fingers, furniture or trim | Refine the selection with Mark, Shift-click and the brush/eraser. |
| Lettering becomes mirrored or reverses after processing | Compare the finished preview and export to the artwork. For the affected shirt, a flipped upload copy compensates for the reversed mapping; keep the master upright and verify the export. A small front-facing mark may work with **Wrap to shape off**, as in the cup example. Reject a result that still differs from the master. |
| The artwork looks pasted flat | Check **Wrap to shape**, wait for processing, then inspect the Shape mode. |
| Letters stretch badly at a crease | Check the base image’s fold and artwork placement. Move the print only if that matches the real product; otherwise use a better view. |
| Pattern scale differs between variants | Compare motif count across the same visible product span and use a sample photograph as the reference. |
| Colors look dull or the print is faint | Check the underlying surface color, blend and opacity. Multiply inherits the substrate’s color. |
| Fabric looks plastic or heavily embossed | Reduce Gloss or Texture and verify **Texture from** matches your source. |
| An old design shows through | Prefer a plain base. Normal can repaint a selected surface, but inspect the result around folds and seams. |
| Preview looks soft | Inspect **High-quality preview** and the exported PNG. Also check the actual source-photo and artwork resolution. |

## Before using the image on a product page

Review the composite beside a photograph of the manufactured item and the approved artwork. Check exact spelling, symbol shape, variant color, motif count, physical print placement, seams and unprinted regions. Then review the intended mobile crop. Keep a clear product view and a detail image in the gallery when the lifestyle image cannot show the design adequately.

[Shopify’s product-photography guidance](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/products/product-media/product-photography) frames product photographs around showing customers what they will receive. Apply that test to the mockup: a new print file does not establish that the pictured material, construction or colorway exists. Label proposed designs clearly when they are still concepts.

An exported mockup is also separate from a production print file. [Printful’s explanation of mockup images](https://help.printful.com/hc/en-us/articles/50264186389393-What-are-mockup-images) makes that distinction explicitly: the mockup is a printing reference, while the correct print file is still required. Keep the original artwork, supplier specifications and physical-sample approval in your production process.

For a collection, record the SKU, artwork version, base image and approved export together. Reuse a scene across print variants only when the physical product is otherwise the same. Each different photograph needs its own selection and placement review; this walkthrough does not establish an automatic batch-mapping workflow.

## Frequently asked questions

### Where is Mockup Lab in Astria?

Click a generated image to open its lightbox, then select **Mockup** or press **M**. The separate `/mockup-lab` page contains prepared examples for trying the controls.

### How do I add a logo to an AI-generated product photo?

Generate the product with a plain surface. Open Mockup from the lightbox, choose Add pattern and upload the logo as a transparent PNG. Click the surface, turn Tile artwork off, then adjust its size, position, wrapping and finish. Save to prompt or download the PNG after checking the result.

### How do I make shirt and bedding mockups?

For a shirt front graphic, use a transparent PNG with Tile artwork off. For a duvet cover and matching pillowcase, use a seamless repeat with Tile artwork on and a separate layer for each piece. Check the manufactured print size, garment seams, bedding folds and unprinted areas before exporting.

### Does Mockup Lab fix misspelled Nano Banana text?

It lets you apply the approved artwork file instead of asking the image model to regenerate those letters. Start with a plain printable area so the old lettering does not show through. The finished print still needs review for size, legibility, shading and distortion.

### Does a mockup prove the product will print correctly?

No. It is a visual composite. Verify the manufacturing file, physical dimensions, material and printed sample separately. Use product photography to confirm details the mockup cannot establish.
