**Reviewed October 15, 2026.** This guide uses product-only methods. It makes no claim about a provider's child-model generation support and includes no generated children.

## Build a product-only shot matrix

Start with the real sample and record its labeled size, SKU, colorway and measurements relevant to the listing. Prepare clothes according to care instructions, remove lint and show hems, straps and cuffs without hiding their shape. Never use an adult body as evidence of a child's fit.

| Buying question | Product-only photograph | Supporting information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is the complete shape? | Straight full front and back flatlays | Labeled size and sample version |
| How does it open? | Unobstructed closure detail, photographed open and closed if needed | Actual button, snap or zipper construction |
| What is the surface like? | Sharp fabric/print closeup | Verified material composition from the product record |
| Is there lining or a separate piece? | Interior and component photographs | What is included in the sold set |
| How long/wide is it? | Measurement view with a clear scale and documented method | Garment measurements and size chart |
| How does the garment hold shape? | Appropriate supported/mannequin view | Support dimensions; no claim of actual wearing fit |

Measure each sample rather than inferring size from the age label or a generated body. Keep the measurement method consistent across the range. A display form shows its own support geometry; it is not proof that a child of a stated age will fit the garment.

## Choose flatlay, hanger or support deliberately

**Flatlay** makes front/back construction accessible and works well for a consistent catalog grid. Keep the camera parallel, show the full garment, and avoid folding sleeves or hems for styling before the evidence set is complete. Use the [phone capture plan](./photograph-clothes-with-phone.md) for setup.

**Hanger** can show a silhouette, but check shoulder distortion and dangling straps. It is useful only when that presentation does not obscure the product's actual proportions.

**Suitable mannequin or support** can reveal volume and neckline construction. Record support dimensions, check stretching and clips, and photograph the garment before retouching away the support. Do not make the garment look narrower or longer to fit an unsuitable form. The [ghost mannequin alternatives guide](./ghost-mannequin-alternative.md) helps choose the format.

**Detail photographs** complete the evidence set. For a two-piece outfit, show both pieces and their individual details. For reversible or lined garments, capture what the customer will actually receive. A styled cover photograph cannot substitute for these views.

## Keep scene editing separate from product evidence

If using AI to create a background or campaign layout, retain the product-only originals as the source of truth. Keep the product shape, print and scale fixed, and check every candidate against them. Product-only compositing can preserve the real garment while changing surrounding props or the surface.

Do not invent wearing fit, comfort, stretch recovery, movement or safety/performance from a still rendering. If the listing requires such evidence, obtain the appropriate physical demonstration and product review, or narrow the claim. An attractive miniature outfit on an adult-proportioned generated figure is not a useful substitute.

Props can also mislead scale. Keep a measured garment view or size chart with the listing rather than relying on a toy, chair or styled scene to imply dimensions.

## Approve and hand off by size and colorway

The product owner compares front/back shape, print, closures, lining and measurements with the actual sample. The creative owner checks consistency and crops after product approval. Reject changed seams, enlarged neck openings, altered inseam length or missing components.

Store source and output files by SKU, colorway, size/sample and view. Record which sample supports each photograph and keep rejected candidates outside delivery. The [catalog kit](./batch-product-photos-from-csv.md) supports the mapping and review records; add size information in the accompanying product brief.

Check the smallest listing crop for cut-off cuffs and closures, and the native-size image for seam/detail accuracy. For a new size, do not reuse a differently sized garment as if it proved identical proportions.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I photograph kids' clothes for my store without organizing a child model shoot?

Use full front/back flatlays, a suitable support where helpful, detail photographs and actual garment measurements. These methods explain product construction without making an unsupported wearing-fit claim.

### Can an adult model or generated body show kidswear fit?

An adult body is not evidence of child fit. Generated proportions also do not establish physical fit, comfort or movement. Use actual product measurements and appropriate physical evidence for those claims.

### Can I use AI just for a kidswear background?

Treat it as a product-only scene edit: retain approved source evidence and check garment shape, color, print, scale and closures. Keep the original product layer where exact preservation is needed.
