## Fermat AI pricing: Pro, Studio, and Enterprise

| Plan | Monthly price, USD | Included credits |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Pro | $30 | 150 |
| Studio | $250 | 500 |
| Enterprise | Custom | Custom |

Fermat's [pricing page](https://www.fermat.app/pricing) lists one credit per image and four per video. Studio includes unlimited users; Enterprise adds API and batch processing, SSO, and custom workflows. The free plan contains one credit. Annual prices are separate from the monthly rates above.

A sample brief of 1,000 generated images and ten videos requires 1,040 credits at those listed rates. That is 540 beyond the Studio allowance before further attempts. Obtain a quote for the extra capacity rather than treating the subscription allowance as a production ceiling. This arithmetic describes generations, not approved deliverables.

## Fermat review: design tools with photography overlap

Fermat's [homepage](https://www.fermat.app/) describes sketch rendering, design changes, material work, and virtual models. It also describes bulk automation and a shared workspace that preserves iterations and decisions. That is more than a folder of exported design files.

The [photography workflow](https://www.fermat.app/use-cases/photography) explicitly covers ecommerce and campaign imagery, garment extraction, model visualisation, background and pose changes, and exports up to 4K. That puts it in direct consideration for a photography brief as well as a design brief.

Our assessment: the breadth is valuable when design and content share inputs. The test is how much setup and correction survives between tools. Can a reviewer trace an image back to the approved garment reference? Can another operator reproduce the treatment? A long tool list answers neither question on its own.

## Enterprise security and IP questions

Fermat publishes SOC 2 Type II, private-model, content-ownership, and SSO/on-premise claims on its [site](https://www.fermat.app/). Treat these as vendor statements and request the current documents and exact plan scope during procurement. This review has not audited the certification or deployment options.

Ask both suppliers how uploaded brand assets are handled, who can access shared work, and how exports and deletion work. Obtain answers for the service and plan you would actually buy. A homepage badge is a starting point for that conversation, not its conclusion.

## Astria: evaluating the final image set

Astria's [fashion offering](https://www.astria.ai/ecommerce) uses product references and templates for lookbook, social, and campaign imagery. Its [API documentation](https://docs.astria.ai/docs/api/tune/create/) is available for teams assessing programmatic production.

The reason to include it in the pilot is the final deliverable: an approved treatment that the team can carry across the collection. The [AI lookbook guide](./ai-lookbook-guide.md) explains the sequence we recommend. Test the same sequence in Fermat rather than assuming a design-led product cannot support it.

## A realistic workflow comparison: two departments, one collection

Imagine that design already uses Fermat and ecommerce is choosing how to produce 250 style-colourways at four images each. The existing design assets are useful inputs. They are not automatically accurate records of the manufactured garments.

Choose five approved products and supply the same front, back, and detail references to both pilots. Require a neutral product image and a directed campaign frame. Change a colourway after approval, then ask a different operator to finish the set. Record which steps need manual repetition and which references can be reused.

If Fermat already clears the quality and handoff test, account for the cost of introducing another tool. If Astria reduces correction or setup work, quantify that saving against the same deliverables. Existing adoption is neither a reason to stop evaluating nor a reason to replace a working process.

## Choose Fermat if...

- Design development is a substantial part of the job.
- The same team needs to move from design references into photography work.
- Its documented enterprise options warrant a procurement review.

## Choose Astria if...

- The immediate brief is a fashion image set from approved products.
- You want to evaluate template-led production alongside a broader design toolbox.
- Your technical team wants to test the documented generation API.

## What to test before choosing either platform

Review the garments at full size and the collection as a contact sheet. Count approved images, manual correction time, and the effort of repeating the treatment after a handoff. Then calculate [cost per SKU](./product-photography-cost-per-sku.md), including people as well as credits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Fermat only a fashion design tool?

No. Its published photography workflow also covers ecommerce and campaign imagery. Evaluate how well that workflow preserves your approved products and creative direction.

### Does Fermat have team and automation options?

Yes. Its published plans include team access and enterprise automation options. Match the required features to the quoted plan before comparing costs.

### Is Astria better than Fermat for fashion brands?

The answer depends on the brief. Compare the same garments, channel outputs, review standard, and operator handoff. This documentation review does not establish an image-quality winner.
