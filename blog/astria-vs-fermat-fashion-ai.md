## Fermat: a toolbox with a procurement-ready wrapper

Fermat calls itself the generative AI toolbox used by global fashion and luxury brands, and its reference list backs the claim: S.Oliver Group, ASOS, Tendam, PUIG, Desigual, Riachuelo, and Children's Apparel Network. That is a serious set of names, and notably they are fashion names—unlike several general creative platforms whose marquee logos come from other industries.

The product is organized as tools rather than as a workflow. More than fifty of them cover sketch-to-render, parametric design modification, extracting graphics and prints from reference imagery, and visualizing garments on models. For ecommerce work specifically, Fermat describes pulling garments out of styled imagery to produce clean flat-lays, then visualizing on models, adjusting backgrounds and poses, and exporting up to 4K. It publishes efficiency claims of 75–80% reduction on core design tasks and 192× faster outfit visualization; both are vendor figures without disclosed methodology.

The differentiator that closes deals is the wrapper. SOC2 Type II, SSO, on-premise deployment options, private models that do not train on customer data, and an explicit statement that generations belong to the customer. For a luxury group whose archive is the crown jewel, that combination is not a nice-to-have.

Pricing is published, which is welcome: roughly $30 a month for 150 credits at the individual tier, $250 a month for 500 credits with unlimited users at the team tier, and custom enterprise. One credit per image, four per video.

## Astria: a professional fashion-production environment

Astria is organized around producing a collection rather than around a toolkit. Products, casting, references, templates, and outputs live in a brand workspace, and the approved treatment—casting, styling, scene, crop, lighting, format—is the reusable unit.

The difference is what happens on the second SKU. A toolbox applies an operation again; a treatment applies a decision again. Astria's templates cover beauty, jewelry, footwear, sports, luxury, and editorial as distinct categories; the casting library spans ages and body types including children and extended sizes; and campaign, editorial, social, and motion all derive from the same approved direction.

## Public proof: design adoption versus production adoption

Fermat's evidence sits with design departments at large fashion groups, which is exactly where its product is aimed and is genuinely strong. Read it as proof that the toolbox earns a place in a luxury design workflow.

Astria's public materials show a production base among fashion brands and the professionals who create for them: Ronny Kobo, Nununu, Gant, Lee Cooper, and Nine West, alongside studios and photographers running client work. Vendor-published in both cases; the difference is which function inside a brand is doing the adopting.

## A realistic workflow comparison: two departments, one collection

Consider a mid-size group where the design team already uses Fermat and the ecommerce team is choosing how to produce the season's imagery.

The design team's experience is good, and the tools fit how designers work: extract a print from a reference, modify a silhouette, visualize the piece on a model before the sample exists. Credits are cheap per image, security cleared review, and the archive stays private.

Extending that into production is where the shape strains. Producing 250 SKUs across PDP, detail, campaign, and social means running tools repeatedly rather than running an approved treatment; the brand's crop and casting conventions live in whoever operates the tools; casting for the children's line and macro conventions for jewelry are outside the library's depth; and when the freelance art director wants to review, the natural unit is exported files rather than a shared canvas. The 500-credit team tier covers 500 images a month, which a 250-SKU catalog at four images each exceeds in a single push.

With Astria, the ecommerce team defines the treatment once with the art director inside the workspace, imports the SKU set from Shopify or Google Drive, and runs it. Campaign, social, and motion extend the same direction. The design team keeps Fermat, because nothing about this replaces sketch-to-render or print extraction.

## The differences that matter most

### Enterprise security and IP posture — winner: Fermat

This is Fermat's genuine edge and the one concession that matters here. SOC2 Type II, SSO, on-premise options, private models that do not train on customer data, and explicit content ownership form a package that many organizations require before any creative AI tool touches an archive. If your procurement review starts there, Fermat has already answered the hard questions and Astria should be asked to document its own terms.

### Toolbox versus treatment — winner: Astria

Fifty tools is impressive breadth and it puts the burden of consistency on the operator. Every asset is the result of choices made again. Astria stores the choice: the approved treatment produces the next SKU the same way as the last one, without anyone remembering how.

### Channel coverage across the season — winner: Astria

Fermat's ecommerce use case produces PDP and campaign images from design assets. A season also needs lookbook, editorial, social-native crops, and motion, consistent with one another. Astria treats those as expressions of a single direction rather than as separate tool runs.

### Casting depth — winner: Astria

Trying designs on different models is a design-validation feature. Production casting is a different requirement: consistent identities across a collection, age ranges including children, extended-size representation, and locations and poses that recur across seasons. Astria's library is built for that.

### Collaboration on the creative decision — winner: Astria

Fermat's team tier is generous—unlimited users—and the interaction model is still tool-and-asset. Astria's multiplayer Board is built for the moment a look gets approved: named cursors, live voice, and the production canvas shared with the photographer, in-house studio, and agency.

### Collection scale and commercial fit — winner: Astria

Credits per image are transparent and easy to reason about, and the published team tier tops out at 500 images a month before enterprise. That suits design exploration and constrains catalog production, where rework is routine. Astria treats the collection as the unit of work and prices to usage—pay for what you create. This article makes no claim about which is cheaper; the meters measure different work. Model a full collection, including rework, before comparing.

### Compounding production value — winner: Astria

In Astria, products, avatars, templates, approvals, and outputs accumulate in a shared workspace, and the next season starts from them. In Fermat, what accumulates is a brand-trained model and a body of design assets — valuable upstream, and not a production workflow.

## Choose Fermat if...

- The buyer is the design department and the work is design: sketch to render, prints, modification.
- SOC2 Type II, SSO, on-premise, or private-model requirements gate any tool you adopt.
- Protecting a brand archive from training use is a stated condition.
- Published per-credit pricing at design-scale volumes fits your usage.

## Choose Astria if...

- The buyer is the team producing the season's imagery.
- One approved creative direction must carry across catalog, lookbook, campaign, social, and motion.
- Casting depth across ages and body types is a production requirement.
- Your photographer, studio, or agency should work inside the brand workspace.
- Catalog volumes exceed what design-scale credit tiers comfortably cover.

## The bottom line

These two are more complementary than competitive, and the split runs along departmental lines. Fermat is an excellent toolbox for a design team, with the security posture a luxury group needs. Astria is the stronger choice for producing the collection's imagery: it stores the creative decision, covers every channel, and scales to a catalog rather than to design exploration.

If your design team already runs Fermat, that is not an argument against Astria. It is an argument for asking which department has the bottleneck.

## What to test before choosing either platform

Run five representative SKUs through both to a full channel set — PDP, detail, campaign, social — and add a colorway after approval. Count credits consumed including rejected frames, and check that number against your real catalog volume. Ask both vendors for written security and IP terms. Have a senior creative review outputs blind for garment fidelity, drape, anatomy, styling, and brand fit. Finally, ask a second operator to reproduce the approved direction.
