## Prepare the garment and the workspace

Steam or press as appropriate for the care instructions, remove lint, close intended fasteners and check that the sample is the version you sell. Lay sleeves, straps and hems so the shape is visible. Photograph a tag or internal catalog card separately to tie the source set to SKU, colorway and size; keep that card out of final listing imagery.

Choose a clean surface large enough for the complete garment. Use stable diffuse light, such as a large shaded window or diffused fixtures, and avoid mixing visibly different light colors. Check the garment for bright glare, clipped highlights, deep shadows and color casts before taking the full batch. A white garment needs enough separation from its background to retain its edges.

## Set the phone by what you can observe

Clean the lens. Use the normal camera rather than a beauty filter, portrait blur or a screenshot. Keep the phone parallel to a flat garment so one side does not look longer. Step back enough to include the whole item, then crop later; avoid an extreme wide-angle view close to the product.

Stabilize the phone on a support or tripod. Tap the product to focus and inspect the saved photograph at full size. If your camera offers exposure or white-balance locking, compare a test before using it for the batch. The useful result is detail without motion blur, clipped white fabric or crushed dark fabric; a single shutter speed or exposure setting cannot guarantee that across rooms and phones.

Do not use screen appearance as final color proof. Compare a neutral reference and the physical garment under consistent viewing light, retain an unfiltered original, and flag difficult reflective, fluorescent or subtle colors for separate capture/review.

## Capture a small evidence set

| Product family | Essential starting views | Details that need their own photograph |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tee or sweatshirt | Full front, back, sleeve shape | Chest print, cuff, label, hem, texture |
| Trousers | Full front and back with waistband and hems visible | Closure, pockets, rise construction and fabric |
| Dress or swimwear | Full front/back, straps untwisted | Lining, gathering, closures, transparency and size information |
| Structured jacket | Front/back on suitable support | Lapels, lining, buttons, seam details and sleeve ends |
| Bag or footwear | Front, side and useful three-quarter view | Hardware, handle/strap, base or sole, measured dimensions |

Take a second view when a fold hides a construction feature. Include a scale photograph or recorded measurements when dimensions affect the brief. Neither a flat lay nor an AI rendering establishes how a size fits a person.

Use a practical source target of at least 1600 pixels on the long edge with clear detail for the intended crop; more pixels do not repair blur. Retain the original full-resolution file rather than exporting through a messaging app.

## Batch without losing the product mapping

Write the required views before setting up, using the [lookbook brief and shot-list example](./how-to-create-a-lookbook.md#stage-3--line-sheet-and-shot-list) and the [pose/framing workshop](./choose-poses-framing-ai-product-photos.md). For product evidence, add any front, back and detail views the creative shot list omits.

Group garments by support and lighting needs: flat tees together, structured pieces together, shiny hardware separately. Approve a test photograph for each group before capturing the rest. Keep the phone position and product marks consistent, and recheck after any light or setup change.

Use this shoot-day order with a small team:

1. A preparation owner checks samples against the SKU list and queues the first category; the camera operator builds and tests its setup.
2. Review one complete garment set together before moving on. The product reviewer checks coverage, color, sharpness and measurements in the saved files.
3. Capture the remaining garments in that setup. Tick off each required view against its SKU/colorway before swapping the item; recheck the test after light changes.
4. Leave difficult materials in a hold queue with a named missing view or failure. Resolve them with a revised setup or targeted professional capture.
5. Copy and count the originals against the shot list before clearing the set or returning samples. One person may fill several roles, but keep this review pause.

Name files by SKU, colorway and view, such as `TEE-01/navy/front.jpg`. Compare the front/back set before putting the sample away. Keep a second copy of originals, then create edited exports separately. The [CSV starter kit](./batch-product-photos-from-csv.md) turns this folder into a reviewed batch plan.

## Choose DIY, studio or reference-led AI

| Approach | Good use | Reason to change approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phone capture | Straightforward product evidence with visible detail | Lighting, color or geometry cannot be made trustworthy |
| Targeted professional capture | Reflective materials, exact color, complex construction, true wearing/fit evidence | Brief and sample are not ready; resolve those first |
| AI from approved references | Scene/background variations and some on-model presentation | Hidden views, labels or fit would need invention |
| Hybrid | Real product layer with edited background and retouch | Composite cannot maintain natural edges, shadow or reflections |

Before converting a garment, follow the [product-reference workshop](./choose-product-reference-photos-ai-fashion.md) and [flat-lay-to-on-model guide](./flat-lay-to-on-model.md). If the source fails, recapture that source; additional generation does not supply missing product truth.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get decent photos of my clothing line when I can't organize a full photoshoot?

Prepare the garment, use a stable diffuse-light setup, keep the phone parallel to the product and capture full views plus details. Test a representative garment and inspect the saved originals before shooting the whole group.

### How can a small fashion brand make its product photos look more professional?

Keep preparation, light, camera position, background and crop consistent across comparable products. Remove lint and distracting folds, preserve visible fabric detail, and review color against the sample. Styling cannot repair a blurry or incomplete source.

### How do I plan a clothing photoshoot when I have lots of styles and only a small team?

Make a SKU/view checklist, group products by setup, and approve one complete set per group. Assign preparation, capture and product review roles, then verify originals before putting each sample away. Keep difficult items in a separate hold queue.

### How do I compare doing product photos myself with hiring a studio or using AI?

Compare the same required views and acceptance standard. DIY phone capture can supply straightforward evidence; a studio can address difficult light, color or construction; AI can extend approved references into reviewed presentation. Include preparation, review and rework when comparing the whole job.

### What should I photograph before sending clothes back to a supplier?

Capture every requested view plus print, closures, texture, labels and measurements needed for review, with a reliable SKU/colorway mapping. A missing hidden view cannot later be verified from a generated image.
