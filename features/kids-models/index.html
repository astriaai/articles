Create models / Kids

Kids fashion models: make childhood—not a grown-up campaign made smaller.

The best kids image has an easy truth to it: the child looks their age, the clothes can move, and nothing feels over-directed. Start with a synthetic cast, build a small world around the collection, then keep the same young character from the first product page to the last lookbook frame.

Make it, step by stepCopy the prompts

The point of view

Cast a character a parent would recognize.

Write down an exact age, a natural expression, hair, a few visible facial traits, and the energy of the brand. “Cool kid” is too vague. “An eight-year-old with a thoughtful half-smile, springy dark curls and a small gap between the front teeth” gives the casting image a personhood without borrowing a real child’s identity.

Keep the styling childlike and ordinary at the casting stage: a plain crew-neck T-shirt, clean daylight, no jewelry, no adult makeup, no glamour pose. The clothing references and art direction arrive after the face is approved. That separation makes it easier to judge the cast instead of being seduced by a finished campaign image.

Before you make anything

Use synthetic identities for children.

Astria’s service is not directed to children under 16 and its privacy policy says it does not knowingly collect their personal information. For this workflow, create a fictional child model rather than uploading a real child’s photographs. An adult brand team should own the account, the brief, and every approval.

Make it

From blank page to a production-ready first set.

Work small until the cast, product and photograph agree. Then make the system repeatable.

  1. 01

    Write the casting card

    Choose exact age, visible features, hair, expression, and brand energy. Describe heritage only when it comes from the brief; do not guess it from where the brand is based.

  2. 02

    Cast in Recraft 4.1

    Generate two square, clean studio portraits with no identity reference. Pick the face for character and age—not for a perfect finish. The Astria unique-headshot skill uses Recraft 4.1 for this first casting pass.

  3. 03

    Turn the winner into the cast

    Register the selected synthetic portrait as a reusable face reference. From here on, the face has an ID; your prompts should not try to reinvent it with adjectives.

  4. 04

    Build a short shot battery

    Start with front, 45-degree, back, and one movement frame. Keep the same age and height/proportion description in every on-model prompt, then check hands, shoes, hems, logos, and whether the expression still feels natural.

Show me the money

The return is a cast you do not have to recast for every SKU.

Approve one fictional child identity, one background, and four useful poses on a small pilot. Once those choices hold, a late colorway or wholesale crop becomes continuation work—not a second production day.
