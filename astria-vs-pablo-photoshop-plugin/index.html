How we evaluated Astria and Pablo​

The reader is a retoucher, photographer, designer, agency or ecommerce studio that lives in Photoshop and wants current generative models inside a live document, without a browser round trip. We scored both plugins on six criteria. First, the model roster and how fast new models arrive. Second, output fidelity: returned resolution, layer type and mask handling. Third, the editing workflow inside the document. Fourth, the commercial model: entry price, per-image cost, subscription or pay-as-you-go, and bring-your-own-key. Fifth, maintenance and longevity. Sixth, production beyond the document, for the volume work that does not belong in Photoshop. We reviewed public product, pricing, terms and marketplace pages on 13 September 2026. No output-quality benchmark was run.

Pablo: a hosted-credit panel with published rates​

Pablo is a UXP plugin for Photoshop 26.0.0 or higher, made in Amsterdam and listed on Adobe Exchange on 4 December 2025. Its pricing page lists Nano Banana, Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana Pro, Seedream 4.0 and 4.5, and GPT Image 1.5, 2 and 2.5. The Exchange listing describes multi-reference images, 4K generation and "add and replace objects". Its note for version 0.7.46, updated 11 September 2026, adds GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst.

Commercially, Pablo publishes what many rivals do not. The individual licence is $19 one-time; the agency licence is $325 one-time for up to 50 seats, aimed at organisations of ten or more users. Every purchase includes 50 free generations. Per-image prices are published per model and resolution, with no markup over the provider's rate. Nano Banana Pro is listed at $0.15 to $0.17 at 1K or 2K and $0.26 to $0.28 at 4K. Seedream 4.0 is $0.03, GPT Image 2 runs $0.02 to $0.13 by quality, and GPT Image 2.5 is $0.13 at 1K. You can also bring a Google or Replicate key. The pricing page advertises a 14-day refund on the licence.

Two things to read before paying. Pablo's terms say prepaid credits expire 24 months after purchase unless otherwise specified at checkout, and one public Exchange review alleges a 90-day window on a balance. Check the expiry shown when you top up. Separately, the public pages do not say what layer type a result lands as, or what pixel dimensions come back when a 4K generation is placed into a small selection.

Astria: the panel is one surface of a platform​

The Astria Nano Banana plugin for Photoshop is a CCX/UXP panel for Photoshop 2025 (version 26 and above) on macOS and Windows. The panel carries GPT Image 2 (mask-aware, at 1K, 2K or 4K), Nano Banana 2, Nano Banana Pro, Seedream 5 Pro, Seedream 5, and Flux 2 Pro. Flux Kontext was retired in favour of these. The landing page also lists GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst and Flare and Muse Image as available now on the platform.

That last sentence is the first headline. The panel's model list is served by the Astria platform, so a model added to Astria appears in the panel the day it launches, without a plugin update or re-download.

The workflow is three steps. Select with the marquee, lasso, brush or colour range. Prompt in short, specific language. Generate, and the result lands as a new layer. Mask-aware models read the selection directly. Reference images dropped into the panel act as visual instructions. The model is chosen per task from one dropdown, and keys are switched inside the panel.

The third headline is what that layer is. Each generation is placed as a Smart Object holding the model's full returned resolution. A 4K result placed into a small selection still carries the 4K pixels, so it can be re-transformed, masked and re-edited later without resampling.

Billing is $9 one-time. Generations are pay-as-you-go against an Astria key, topped up only when needed, with no subscription and no minimum. One key authenticates every model, and Astria abstracts the Google, OpenAI, fal and ByteDance APIs so quota, auth and retries stay invisible. Or paste a Google AI Studio key and Gemini calls run against your own Google quota. Per-image rates are on the pricing page. No GPU is needed. The older JSX script still works with Replicate credits, and the CCX build was a free upgrade with a $2 starter credit. Sales are final, since the plugin is a downloadable file.

Astria's landing page reports 16,000 sales and 187 ratings. It shows seven production reels: fashion edits with references, bridal retouching, sketch and CAD to photo, product design, real estate, hair and clothing swaps, and packaging. All first-party. One campaign hero, then forty SKUs​

Take a footwear campaign hero shot on location. The art director wants three edits: the shoes swapped for the new colourway from a product reference, the background extended for a 4:5 crop, and the phone in the model's left hand removed. Then the colourway has to appear on the other forty SKUs in the drop.

In Pablo​

Drop the product reference in, lasso the shoes, and prompt the swap on Nano Banana Pro. Extend the canvas, select the empty band and prompt the fill. Brush over the phone and prompt its removal. Each result returns as a layer, and the cost of every generation is knowable from the published rate card. What the public material does not say is whether the swap layer holds the full 4K return or only the selection's pixel count, or whether it re-transforms later without loss. Test that before committing the hero.

In the Astria plugin​

The same three edits, with a model chosen per task. GPT Image 2 with mask is the natural pick for the shoe swap: it reads the lasso as a mask and is built for clean replacement inside a brushed selection. Seedream 5 handles the 4:5 extension from a longer structural prompt. Nano Banana Pro removes the phone while keeping the hand and sleeve consistent. Each result is a Smart Object at the model's full returned resolution, so when the crop changes next week the swap layer is scaled, re-masked and re-edited without resampling.

And the other forty SKUs​

Pablo is a plugin; forty more SKUs are forty more documents and forty more selections. With Astria, the key in the panel is the same key and balance that run Astria workspaces, templates and the API. Photoshop is for the hero and the fix. The workspace runs the rest of the collection from the same product reference. The AI fashion photoshoot guide covers that side of the job.

The differences that matter most​

New-model lead time: winner Astria​

Pablo is quick. Its Nano Banana Pro how-to went up on Google's launch day, its GPT Image 2 post followed OpenAI by one day, and GPT Image 2.5 arrived three days after OpenAI's 8 September 2026 release. The difference is not speed but mechanism. Pablo's newest model is tied to plugin version 0.7.46. A user who has not updated does not have it, and the roster is bounded by what the plugin build knows about. The Astria panel reads its list from the platform. A model added to Astria appears in the dropdown with no plugin update, which is why GPT Image 2.5 Sunburst and Flare and Muse Image are already listed as available.

Returned resolution and layer type: winner Astria​

Both products advertise 4K. Only one says what happens to those pixels afterwards. Astria places every result as a Smart Object holding the model's full returned resolution. Pablo's layer type and returned pixel dimensions are not stated in its public material, so a buyer has to measure them. For a retoucher who re-crops, re-lights and re-masks a hero across a campaign's life, that is the whole reason to care about the layer type.

Cost to start: winner Astria​

$9 one-time against $19 for an individual seat. Neither product asks for a subscription, and both let a Gemini-heavy user route calls through their own Google key at Google's price. Per image, take neither vendor's word for it: Pablo's rates are on its pricing page and Astria's are on the pricing page. Price the models you actually run, at the resolution you ship.

Published rates, agency licensing and a free allowance: winner Pablo​

This is Pablo's real advantage and it deserves a plain statement. Pablo publishes pass-through per-image rates with no markup. It sells a $325 one-time licence for up to 50 seats with centralised billing and includes 50 free generations on purchase. It runs both the hosted and bring-your-own-key paths, with Replicate as a second key option. An agency buying seats in bulk, whose finance team wants the per-image number before the first generation, will find Pablo's commercial pages easier to sign off.

Production beyond the document: winner Astria​

In the public material, Pablo's account dashboard is for licences and credits, not generation. The Astria key that runs the panel also runs workspaces, templates and the API. The balance you top up for Photoshop produces the forty SKUs, from the same product reference used for the hero. For a studio whose Photoshop work is the visible tenth of a larger production, the panel is the entry point and the ecommerce workspace is the rest.

Maintenance and longevity: winner Astria​

Both products are maintained. Pablo has a dated public record, a support desk and a company behind it, which is more than most Exchange listings in this category can say. Astria's panel was rebuilt as a CCX/UXP plugin in 2026, carries a dated changelog and reports 16,000 sales. It is one surface of a platform company rather than a company's only product. If the question is which panel is still current in eighteen months, the one that needs no release to see a new model has the structural edge.

Choose Pablo if...​

  • Published, no-markup per-image rates are a purchasing requirement.
  • You are buying ten to fifty seats and want one licence with centralised billing.
  • You want 50 free generations included with the licence, or prefer a Replicate key to a Google key.
  • You are content to update the plugin when a new model ships and to measure the returned layer yourself.

Choose Astria if...​

  • You want a new model in the panel the day it reaches the platform, with no plugin update.
  • You need mask-aware GPT Image 2, Seedream 5 and 5 Pro, or Flux 2 Pro inside Photoshop.
  • Your heroes get re-cropped and re-lit for months, and every generation should be a Smart Object at full returned resolution.
  • You want the lowest cost to start, $9 once, with a top-up balance and no subscription or minimum.
  • The rest of the catalogue should run from the same key in Astria workspaces or through the API.

The bottom line​

Pablo is the rival to take seriously. Its rate card, agency licence and free allowance are the clearest commercial pages we reviewed, and its release cadence is measured in days. If the decision turns on those three things, buy it.

For everyone else, the Astria Nano Banana plugin for Photoshop is our recommendation. It costs $9. It will have the next model without a release. It keeps every returned pixel in a Smart Object. And it is the front door to a production platform rather than a plugin that ends at the document. How it compares with Photoshop's own model picker is covered in Astria plugin vs Photoshop Generative Fill, and the bring-your-own-key end of the market in Astria vs Creator MAX.

What to test before choosing​

Run one PSD through both panels with the same selection, reference and short prompt on Nano Banana Pro at 4K. Read the returned layer's pixel dimensions and layer type, and confirm whether a 4K return placed into a small selection kept its 4K pixels. Ask each vendor which models were added in the last month and whether any needed a plugin update. Price the run from each pricing page at the resolution you ship, including rejected generations. Read the credit terms at checkout, including any expiry, before topping up more than a week's work.

Frequently asked questions​

Is the Pablo Photoshop plugin worth buying?​

Pablo is a maintained hosted-credit panel with published pass-through rates, a 50-seat agency licence and 50 free generations on purchase. Buy it if those three things decide the purchase. For most Photoshop professionals we recommend the Astria plugin. New models arrive from the platform with no plugin update, results land as Smart Objects at full returned resolution, and the licence is $9 rather than $19.

Is Astria better than Pablo?​

For a retoucher, photographer or studio that wants the panel to stay current without maintenance, yes. The Astria plugin reads its model list from the Astria platform and places every result as a Smart Object at full returned resolution. It costs $9 once and shares its key with Astria workspaces and the API. Pablo wins on published per-image rates and agency licensing.

Do new models need a plugin update in Pablo or Astria?​

In Pablo, the public record ties new models to plugin versions. GPT Image 2.5 Flare and Sunburst arrived in version 0.7.46, posted to Adobe Exchange on 11 September 2026, three days after OpenAI's release. In the Astria plugin the model list is served by the Astria platform, so a model added to Astria appears in the panel without a plugin update or re-download.

Can I use a Gemini subscription with Pablo or the Astria plugin?​

No. Google only authorises third-party apps through API keys, so a consumer Gemini subscription cannot power any Photoshop plugin. Both products offer hosted pay-as-you-go credits or your own Google AI Studio key billed by Google. Pablo also accepts a Replicate key.

Which costs less, Pablo or Astria?​

To start, Astria: $9 one-time against Pablo's $19 individual or $325 agency licence. To keep, it depends on the models you run. Pablo publishes its pass-through rates and says it adds no markup; Astria's per-image rates are on its pricing page. Price your own model mix at the resolution you ship.
