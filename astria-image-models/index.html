Which AI Image Model Should You Use in Astria? (2026 Guide)

September 7, 2026 · 8 min read
Astria Team
AI fashion production

The short answer: start with Nano Banana 2 for fashion. Choose GPT Image 2 when beauty art direction or readable text is central, Seedream 5 for swimwear and demanding color, texture, or identity work, and Muse when several references must survive one complicated brief.

That is the useful model hierarchy. It is not a claim that one model wins every image prompt.

These recommendations come from a controlled Astria comparison captured on September 6, 2026: 11 briefs, four models, three requested outputs per brief, and 131 returned images. See the full visual benchmark and methodology.

Nano Banana 2: the fashion default​

Nano Banana 2 is the answer when a creative team should not have to become a model-routing team before it can make a lookbook.

Across the general fashion, movement, garment-reference, and multi-reference briefs, it most consistently landed in the middle of the useful production triangle: the image looked directed, the references remained recognizable, and the composition was close to something an art director could continue with. Some specialists matched or exceeded it in individual tests. None displaced it as the safest starting point across ordinary fashion work.

Matched 3:4 brief and references. Each card is the first predetermined output, not a hand-picked favorite.

Use Nano Banana 2 first for:

  • fashion editorials and campaign scenes;
  • moving from a garment reference to an on-model image;
  • shot-list variety with one cast identity;
  • mixed creative and preservation briefs where no single constraint dominates.

GPT Image 2: beauty and text​

GPT Image 2 is the specialist to try when the frame has to behave like an advertisement, not only a photograph. In our beauty test it combined a polished portrait, a held cosmetic product, open copy space, and the exact headline COLOR, KEPT TRUE especially well.

The distinction matters. Exact wording, hierarchy, product geometry, and beauty finish are separate failure points. A model can produce beautiful skin and still miss the headline; it can spell the headline correctly and quietly redesign the bottle. GPT Image 2 gave the most convincing overall beauty-ad composition in this set, although Nano Banana 2 and Muse were competitive and also rendered the large headline correctly.

Choose GPT Image 2 first for:

  • cosmetic campaigns and close beauty portraits;
  • packaging-led advertising;
  • headlines, signs, labels, and designed copy space;
  • layouts where typography is part of the image rather than added later.

For a product listing, still compare every generated label against the real pack. Readable-looking small type is not proof of exact type.

Seedream 5: swimwear, color, texture, and identity​

Seedream 5 is the practical specialist for two different reasons.

First, it is a useful alternate route for ordinary adult swimwear and other briefs that can encounter stricter sensitivity handling elsewhere. This is not advice to bypass safeguards. Keep the subject adult, the framing commercial, and the pose unambiguous. If a benign job still does not complete as expected, Seedream can keep the production moving.

Second, its visual strengths are real. The test showed excellent cobalt and coral separation, rich teal and burnt-orange color, convincing jacquard and fabric sheen, and stable identity across close-up, seated, full-length, and movement shots.

In this particular benchmark every model produced tasteful swimwear images. One Nano Banana 2 editorial request returned two images rather than three, without a reported error or moderation reason. That isolated omission is recorded; it is not presented as proof of why the third image was absent.

Choose Seedream 5 first for:

  • swimwear and skin-exposure briefs with ordinary commercial intent;
  • saturated color and exact color relationships;
  • embroidery, woven surfaces, sheen, and other difficult textures;
  • repeated casting where face and skin continuity matter.

Muse: reference-first, constraint-heavy work​

Muse is the option for the brief that reads like a small logic problem: use this person, that jacket, this necklace, one bottle in the right hand, a specified background, and no extra objects.

We call it a thinking model in a practical editorial sense: its useful distinction is the behavior visible in the output—careful interpretation of multiple references and their separate roles. This guide does not make an undocumented claim about its internal architecture.

Muse was particularly dependable for jewelry and the complex multi-reference frame. It is worth trying when reference reliability matters more than discovering the fastest general-purpose look.

Choose Muse first for:

  • several references with explicit roles;
  • jewelry and accessory placement;
  • strict inclusion and exclusion lists;
  • a reference-led brief that other models keep simplifying or blending.

What else is in Astria's model GUI?​

Astria's popular model selector changes as endpoints improve. In the September 6 catalog snapshot, it included current variants from the Nano Banana and Seedream families alongside Flux 2 Pro, GPT Image 2, Recraft V4.1, Ideogram V4, and WAN 2.7/Pro.

The public partner-model gallery was broader: Reve, Riverflow, HyperRealism, Flux Turbo and Klein, P-Image, Recraft variants, Grok Imagine, Krea, Qwen Image, MAI Image, and Muse were among the visible families.

Two cautions make that list easier to use:

  1. Public in the GUI does not mean open weights. It means the model is visible as a public Astria partner model.
  2. A long catalog is not a reason to test everything on every job. Start with the four-model routing table, then qualify a challenger only when it offers a relevant capability, speed, price, or aesthetic.

Model names and availability are volatile. Treat this as a dated snapshot and check the live selector before committing a production pipeline.

A simple routing workflow​

  1. Run the approved brief in Nano Banana 2.
  2. If the job is beauty- or text-led, run GPT Image 2 alongside it.
  3. If color, texture, identity, or swimwear completion is the risk, add Seedream 5.
  4. If several references must be interpreted separately, add Muse.
  5. Judge product accuracy separately from aesthetic preference.
  6. Approve a treatment before multiplying it across a collection.

The cheapest model per generated image is not automatically the cheapest production choice. The metric that matters is cost per approved output after retries and retouching. The benchmark's 131 images cost $43.15 for the four-model core run; source creation and calibration brought the full project generation spend to $45.79.
