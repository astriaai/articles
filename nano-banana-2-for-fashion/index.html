Nano Banana 2 for Fashion and Product Photography: Tested Guide

September 9, 2026 · 6 min read
Astria Team
AI fashion production

Nano Banana 2 is our default AI image model for fashion work in Astria. It did not win every specialist test. It earned the recommendation by producing the best overall balance across everyday fashion direction, garment use, identity, movement, and complex references.

The verdict​

QuestionAnswer from our test
Best forGeneral fashion editorials, lookbooks, garment-to-model work, movement, and mixed briefs
Main advantageNo single weakness dominates the ordinary fashion workflow
Watch forSpecialist models can beat it on exact text, difficult texture, swimwear completion, or reference-first work
Switch toGPT Image 2 for beauty/text; Seedream 5 for texture or swimwear; Muse for strict multi-reference work; MAI for qualifying direct commercial edits

The controlled benchmark ran 11 briefs with three requested outputs per model. Nano Banana 2 returned 32 of 33 requested images; one poolside swimwear cell returned two images without a reported reason. The article displays the first predetermined output from each completed cell, not a hand-picked favorite.

Why it is the fashion default​

A useful default has to reduce routing decisions. Nano Banana 2 followed the walking fashion brief, preserved the cast and referenced tank, rendered believable trouser movement, and produced a composition ready for refinement. GPT Image 2 gave a polished alternative; Seedream 5 handled color beautifully; Muse read references carefully. Nano Banana 2 was the best-balanced starting point across the complete set.

Use it first for:

  • fashion campaign and lookbook concepts;
  • placing a referenced garment on a model;
  • changing crop, pose, or scene while keeping the overall treatment;
  • identity-led shot lists;
  • briefs mixing creative direction with several preservation requirements.

Product and texture fidelity​

Nano Banana 2 kept the orange jacket recognizable while giving it a credible campaign treatment.

That distinction is worth preserving. Nano Banana 2 is good at the whole frame. Seedream 5 is the model we would add when fabric sheen, saturated color, embroidery, or weave is the approval risk. Product fidelity should be scored separately from beauty: a more attractive picture can still be the wrong SKU.

Identity and movement​

The four-shot series changed camera distance and pose while keeping one cast identity, jacket, setting, and light. Nano Banana 2 maintained a recognizable person from close-up to full length, seated, and walking. The movement frame was especially useful because the body, garment, and face all had to remain plausible at once.

For a real collection, four images are only a qualification. Approve the cast across the actual shot list before generating hundreds of assets.

Complex references​

The stress test combined a person, jacket, necklace, serum bottle, hand placement, background colors, and a no-extras list.

Nano Banana 2 is often enough for multi-reference work. Add Muse when repeated generations keep omitting, blending, or simplifying assets. The decision is not “creative versus accurate”; it is which failure creates more rework on this job.

Where Nano Banana 2 is not first choice​

  • Beauty advertising and prominent copy: start with GPT Image 2.
  • Swimwear, saturated color, and difficult fabric: start with Seedream 5.
  • Jewelry or several strict references: start with Muse Image.
  • Fast text-led exploration or one-image product edits: qualify MAI-Image-2.6 or Flash.

A prompt structure that works​

Create a [shot type] of <person reference> wearing <product reference>. Specify pose, crop, scene, and light. List the product attributes and identity details that must remain unchanged. End with exclusions: no text, logos, extra people, or unrequested accessories.

Avoid burying the product requirements under camera jargon. Start with the role of each reference, then state the creative direction. A model can only be evaluated fairly when the brief defines what counts as success.

Bottom line​

Start with Nano Banana 2 when you do not yet know which specialist you need. Keep it when the result preserves the product, identity, and direction. Switch only when a specific failure—text, texture, swimwear completion, or reference fidelity—points to a better route.
